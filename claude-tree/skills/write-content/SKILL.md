## Step 1: Figure out what's actually being asked for

Ask if it isn't already clear from the request:

- **Platform.** Joe prefers to specify this per request rather than have the skill assume. If he doesn't say, ask which platform(s) — don't default to writing all four.
- **Topic or angle**, if he hasn't given one. Check the bank's real substance section first — suggest 2-3 angles from real, current material; a hypothetical only if nothing real exists and Joe confirms it for this post.

Don't over-interview for a single post — this isn't the calendar-building process. One clarifying question is usually enough; move to drafting.

**Do not invent a hypothetical example (a fictional "a practice falls in love with a space" type scenario) when real material is sitting unused in the content-inspiration-bank, or obtainable by just asking Joe for one.** This was the single biggest problem with the first test batch — the due diligence post used a made-up scenario when it should have used something real.

## Step 2: Which voice — local team, or national CARR?

**Default to the local Joe & Dell voice, on Facebook and Instagram especially.** CARR's national scale and "nation's leading provider" framing is backup credibility, not the lead. Reach for it only to back a specific claim, never as the opening or headline of a post.

Local-first means: name the actual city or corridor (Pensacola, Mobile, Destin, Panama City, Fort Walton Beach, Dothan, Tallahassee) rather than saying "our area," and write like two specific people who work this market, not like a franchise location.

→ **Revised: this local-first default doesn't hold evenly across all four platforms.**

- **Twitter/X has a national/global reach, not a local one.** Don't anchor posts to a specific city ("Pensacola practice owners...") — write for healthcare practice owners generally. The audience reading a tweet isn't primarily Panhandle locals the way a Facebook post's audience is.
- **LinkedIn is a mix of local and national.** Joe's LinkedIn network skews toward vendors, referral partners, and other professionals rather than practice owners themselves (see Step 5) — so it's fine, and sometimes better, to lean on CARR's broader credibility here rather than hyper-local specificity every time.
- **Facebook and Instagram stay local-first**, per the general rule above — this is where the Panhandle specificity does the most work.

## Step 6: Formatting defaults

- **Emojis:** occasional, sparing. Fine for warmth or emphasis on Facebook/Instagram; use less on LinkedIn and Twitter/X. Never more than one or two in a post.
- **Hashtags:** use them when they'd actually help — don't force a row of hashtags onto every post. Location tags (#Pensacola, #FloridaPanhandle) fit Facebook and Instagram, where the audience is genuinely local. On Twitter/X, skip location-specific tags in favor of broader ones (#HealthcareRealEstate, #PracticeOwner) since that audience isn't Panhandle-specific (see Step 2).
- **Length:** short paragraphs (2-4 sentences), no walls of text. LinkedIn can run slightly longer than the others if the topic needs room to land, but should still read easily on a phone.
- **CTA:** → **Revised.** On LinkedIn, Facebook, and Instagram, not every post needs one — skip it when it doesn't fit naturally rather than manufacturing one. On **Twitter/X specifically, default to ending with a real question or discussion prompt** almost every time. X is about interaction; a post that doesn't invite a reply leaves the platform's value on the table. The question should be genuine (something a practice owner or aspiring practice owner would actually want to answer), never generic engagement bait like "thoughts?"

## Step 7: Substance — pillars and Joe's actual opinions

Pull from these three pillars (same ones used in the broader content calendar, so a one-off post stays consistent with it):

1. **Educational / market authority** — lease vs. buy, due diligence pitfalls, what free rent and TI allowances mean for a P&L, what changes about the process when it's a second or third location instead of a first.
2. **Local market activity** — what's happening in a specific Panhandle submarket right now.
3. **Brand / behind-the-scenes** — CARR's no-conflict model, our vendor network (first person; solo-Joe by default — see Guardrails), the "helping people who help people" framing.

**Which awareness stage each pillar tends to hit (problem-unaware → ready-to-hire framework):** don't rotate the pillars purely for variety — each lands differently by where the reader is:
- **Educational / market authority** mostly reaches people who are problem-aware or solution-aware — they already know they have a lease/space decision coming and are evaluating how to think about it, not yet comparing specific brokers.
- **Local market activity** is the widest top-of-funnel reach — useful for staying visible to people who aren't actively thinking about a move yet (problem-unaware), since it's just local news, not a pitch.
- **Brand / behind-the-scenes**, especially the conflict-of-interest angle, is aimed at solution-aware and ready-to-hire readers — people who already know they need representation and are deciding what kind.

Use this as a light check when picking a topic for a specific goal (e.g., if Joe wants something that reaches people who haven't started thinking about a move yet, local market activity is the better pick than a conflict-of-interest hot take) — not as a rigid rule that overrides a good real-substance-bank input.

## Guardrails — do not cross these

- **Standing team fact:** this is our vendor network, shared by Joe and Dell. Dell's 15+ years of healthcare experience is his differentiator. This fact applies to internal work as well as marketing.
- **Solo-Joe by default — do not mention Dell (HARD BAN, Joe, Jul 16 — a live post shipped violating it).** All of Joe's marketing is just Joe unless he explicitly directs partner-framing for a specific piece; Joe and Dell each market individually. Do not name Dell or frame the work as a Joe-and-Dell partnership, and never credit anything (especially the vendor network) to Dell's tenure. Any construction crediting the network's existence or trustworthiness to Dell's tenure is banned; the network is "our network" / "my network," first person, never "Dell's network." Joint pieces are opt-in, on Joe's direction only. Full rule in DNA/writing-rules.md.
- **Never name a specific competing brokerage or firm.** Describe the problem (unrepresented buyers, dual agency, a broker who also takes listings) without naming names.
- **Don't write conflict-of-interest content for LinkedIn by default.** Real listing brokers Joe does deals with are LinkedIn connections, and LinkedIn there skews toward vendors/referral partners rather than practice owners anyway (see Step 5) — so this isn't really the right audience for that topic even set aside the relationship risk. If Joe specifically asks for a conflict-of-interest post on LinkedIn, that's his call to make — write it in the safe "we/educational" register from Step 3, not a personal callout.
- **Never reference an identifiable current prospect or deal**, even anonymized — pipeline volume is too low for anonymization to protect anyone; keep examples hypothetical or industry-general. Revisit once volume grows.
- **No political or social-issue content**, even adjacent. Stay inside real estate and business opinions.
- **HIPAA:** no patient data, no patient-identifying photography, no heat-mapping or demographic content in social posts, ever. That data stays in 1:1 client deliverables.
- **Generated / AI imagery:** allowed, always vision-checked — the hard lines live in Step 8's Vision-check section. HIPAA stays absolute.

## Step 8: Generate the graphic

Joe wants a real visual on most posts, and wants it to look genuinely premium — not like a generic real-estate agent's feed. There are now **three graphic engines** plus real brand assets. Pick the right one per post (Step 8.0), produce it, then run the shared vision-check before delivery. **Never hand Joe a graphic sight-unseen.**

**Brand spec — source of truth is `DNA/brand-voice.md`; put it into every engine, spelled out:** primary navy `#002F6C`, accent orange `#F57F29`, white / near-white background; Oswald (condensed) for headlines, Montserrat for body. **Never rely on a saved Canva Brand Kit for color/font** — kits can hold stale branding and can't be inspected (a past batch quietly pulled wrong colors from a stale kit). Do not pass `brand_kit_id` unless Joe confirms a specific kit is current.

Before generating in any engine, check `DNA/Marketing/Social Media/content-inspiration-bank.md`'s **Visual style notes** for concrete layout/format patterns Joe has liked. If that section is empty, don't assume a plain card is a safe default — ask Joe for a reference example if the stakes justify it.

### Step 8.0 — Pick the engine

**Before defaulting to a text card:** check `content-inspiration-bank.md` Section 3 for the backdrop concepts (territory-map for regional posts, aerial-city duotone for local ones) and the creative menu. Joe's standing direction (Jul 7 2026): use real visuals — a duotoned city aerial, a data-viz big number, a myth/reality split — not just text on a color field, whenever the post gives a reason to.

- **Code-built brand card — DEFAULT for text-forward posts** (a quote/hot-take line, a stat, a short tip or list). Highest-fidelity, most on-brand option: exact hex, real fonts, zero garbled text, free, reproducible. Use it unless the post specifically needs a photo or motion. - **Canva — when the post wants a designed / photo-led layout** the card template doesn't cover (a photo background, a richer multi-element composition, or when Joe wants Canva variety). Template-based; refine with the editing layer (Step 8B).
- **Blotato template visual — when the post wants multiple slides or motion**: a carousel (e.g., "3 things that get missed" as swipeable slides), an infographic, or a short AI video. Template-based with color/font inputs.
- **Photoreal scene — via manual Firefly (Joe's Adobe plan), see Step 8D**: when a post wants an actual photographic/illustrated scene beyond a card or a Canva layout. Joe generates it himself in Firefly (keeps Adobe's IP indemnification; the no-API rationale lives in 8D).

One graphic per post unless Joe asked for multi-platform (then size one per platform — see Output format). If a post genuinely needs no visual (rare), say so rather than making a throwaway.

### Step 8A — Code-built brand card (default)

1. **Copy the template into the workspace** — the skill mount is read-only, so work on a copy (e.g. `/tmp/card/`). Bring `brand-card.html`, the `fonts/` folder, and the `assets/` folder (real CARR logos + Joe's circular avatar — the footer renders avatar + name + CARR logo, white-on-navy / navy-on-light by theme).
2. **Fonts (once per session, do this first):** exact brand type needs Oswald + Montserrat. If `graphics/fonts/Oswald.ttf` + `Montserrat.ttf` exist, copy them beside the template. If not, try installing to `~/.fonts` + `fc-cache` — but package registries and Google Fonts are blocked in some sandboxes; if the fetch fails, the template falls back to a close stack (Poppins / condensed system faces) and the vision-check must confirm the type still reads clean. Exact fonts are a one-time drop into `graphics/fonts/` (see its README) — offer to do it in any session that has network.
3. **Edit only the `DATA` block** (between the two `/* ===== */` sentinels): set `layout` (`quote` | `stat` | `tip`), `theme` (`navy` | `light`), `kicker`, and that layout's text fields; leave `name`/`role` as-is. Keep copy tight — these are cards, not paragraphs (quote ≤ ~18 words; tip ≤ 3 points). **Upgrades (from the CARR brand-post batch — see content-inspiration-bank Section 3):** `accent` (`frame` default | `corner` | `none`), `numbered:true` for a "01 // 02 //" list, `bgImage` for a duotone navy-washed photo background (pairs with the Firefly path, Step 8D), and wrap any phrase in `**…**` to highlight it orange.
4. **Render at 2× with the preinstalled headless Chromium** (`/opt/pw-browsers/chromium-*/chrome-linux/chrome`). CRITICAL: headless Chromium reserves ~87 CSS px of window chrome, so the real viewport is SHORTER than `--window-size` and the bottom of the card (photo backdrop + frame border) gets cut off, leaving a solid navy band at the bottom edge. FIX: render into a window ~160 px TALLER than the card, then crop the screenshot back to the exact target from the top-left (the card is anchored at top:0, so a top-left crop is always the full card):
   `chrome --headless=new --no-sandbox --hide-scrollbars --force-color-profile=srgb --force-device-scale-factor=2 --window-size=1080,1510 --screenshot=raw.png file:///tmp/card/brand-card.html`
   then crop to 2× the target: `python3 -c "from PIL import Image; Image.open('raw.png').convert('RGB').crop((0,0,2160,2700)).save('out.png')"`
   Platform sizing — pick target, set window height = target height + 160, crop = 2× target: IG / LinkedIn / Facebook feed → target 1080×1350 (4:5), window 1080×1510, crop 2160×2700; X → set `DATA.width/height` to 1080/1080, window 1080×1240, crop 2160×2160; stories → target 1080×1920, window 1080×2080, crop 2160×3840. ALWAYS confirm the exported PNG has no solid band at the bottom edge before delivering.
5. Vision-check (below), then deliver the PNG.

### Step 8B — Canva, with the editing / refinement layer

1. `generate-design` with `design_type` matching the platform (`instagram_post`, `facebook_post` — also use for LinkedIn, which has no dedicated type — `twitter_post`). Put the full brand spec in the `query` every time, plus the post's key line, and steer *away* from the real-estate clichés (stock skyline, house/key icons, headshot-with-phone card). No `brand_kit_id`.
2. `create-design-from-candidate` on the chosen candidate → editable `design_id`.
3. **Refine with an editing transaction** (`start-editing-transaction` → `perform-editing-operations` → `commit-editing-transaction`). This is what lifts Canva from "template" to "on-brand":
   - **Fix text exactly** — `replace_text` / `find_and_replace_text` so the copy is Joe's exact wording (kills the garbled/misspelled AI in-image text, Canva's #1 defect).
   - **Set text color to brand hex** — `format_text` with `color:#002F6C` or `#F57F29`, plus size / weight / alignment.
   - **Swap in a real image** — `upload-asset-from-url` to get an `asset_id`, then `update_fill` to drop a real photo (or a code-built PNG) into an image element.
   - Delete junk elements; reposition / resize on fixed-page designs.
   - **Honest limits — don't design around promises the API can't keep:** it can't change the **font family** (only size/weight/style), can't set a shape/background **fill to a hex** (only swap in an image), and on Canva's **responsive** auto-layouts only text/image swaps and deletes work (no reposition/resize/recolor). If exact brand color or type is essential and Canva can't reach it, switch to a code-built card.
   - **`commit-editing-transaction` needs Joe's approval and is irreversible** — show him the change and ask before committing (treat it like a send/publish action).
4. `get-export-formats` → `export-design` (PNG). Vision-check, deliver.

### Step 8C — Blotato template visual (carousel / infographic / motion)

1. `blotato_list_visual_templates` (optionally `search`) to find one matching the format — quote card, tutorial carousel, infographic, narrated/AI video.
2. `blotato_create_visual` with the `templateId` and a descriptive `prompt`. First pass can leave `inputs:{}` and let the prompt drive; for brand control pass `inputs` (colors `#002F6C` / `#F57F29`, fonts, slide count) once you know that template's inputs.
3. Poll `blotato_get_visual_status` until done → returns `imageUrls` (slides) / `mediaUrl` (video).
4. Vision-check each slide, then deliver. (Those URLs can feed `blotato_create_post`'s `mediaUrls` if Joe later wants to publish — but publishing is a separate, approval-required action, never automatic.)

### Vision-check — applies to every engine, never skip

Actually view the exported image(s) and grade against three things: the **brand spec** (navy/orange, right fonts), plain **legibility** (text not cut off, readable at phone size, no garbled or misspelled words), and the **cliché test** (would it blend into the rejected quote-card batch, or any generic agent's feed — skyline, house/key, headshot card?). If it fails any, fix it — code card: adjust `DATA`/sizes and re-render; Canva: another editing pass; Blotato: regenerate with different inputs — rather than delivering with a caveat.

**Generated / AI imagery — open, but vision-checked (Joe's call, July 6 2026):** realistic-style generated visuals are allowed, but every one gets viewed and graded before delivery, and regenerated if it looks off-brand or uncanny. Hard lines that don't move: **no fabricated identifiable people, nothing that reads as a real patient or a specific real practice's space, no invented real building/place** — plus the standing HIPAA rule (no patient data or patient-identifying imagery, ever). Abstract and generic-scene imagery is the safe zone; a fake-looking "doctor stock photo" undercuts a credibility brand — reject it.

## Pre-Delivery Self-Check (added July 2, 2026)

**Run the linter first — it is a gate, not a suggestion (added July 25, 2026).** In a LOCAL Claude Code session, before any judgment pass and before the post reaches Joe, write the draft copy to a scratch file and run:

```bash
~/carr-system/run.sh lint /path/to/draft.txt --surface social
```

A non-zero exit means at least one HARD finding. **Do not hand Joe a draft that has not returned PASS on hard bans.** Fix and re-run until it does. REVIEW findings do not block automatically, but every one has to be looked at and consciously cleared — they are hard-ban rules whose pattern has legitimate uses in CRE prose (literal land, leverage as a noun, landscaping, a market shift). RATION flags two or more of the rationed filler adverbs; cut down to one. In a Cowork/cloud session the linter is not reachable (`~/carr-system` is local-only), so do the same pass by hand against `DNA/writing-rules.md` and say in the handback that the check was manual. Why this exists: the ban list was a document Claude was supposed to remember, and it kept shipping violations that Joe caught after the fact (em-dashes in two diagram documents, the compressed contrast-reframe on Jul 14, the third Dell-attribution correction on Jul 16). Evidence, not confidence.

**Zero-tolerance pass first (added July 6, 2026):** the linter covers the mechanically checkable part of this. The rest is still yours to catch. Before the judgment-based checks below, run the post against `DNA/writing-rules.md`'s Zero-Tolerance AI-Tell List (em dash — now banned in social copy too, not just outreach — flagged vocabulary like "delve"/"seamless"/"unlock," the contrast-reframe construction, rule-of-three overuse, generic AI openers, corporate clichés). Those are hard bans: a single instance is disqualifying no matter how well the rest of the post reads, because the risk is a scrolling reader pattern-matching and dismissing the whole post, not a writing-quality judgment call. The checks below are judgment calls on top of that, not a substitute for it.

Run the copy through these checks (Joe-approved writing-craft prompts; the same 1:1 outreach checklist as DNA/templates.md, adapted for social). They catch the tells that make writing sound AI-generated — a voice pass, not a grammar check:

- **Credibility Test:** read it as a skeptical practice owner would. Cut anything too safe, too vague, or too perfectly hedged. The post should sound like Joe (or Joe & Dell) actually holds the view being expressed, not like a neutral account trying not to offend anyone.
- **Voice Shaper:** cut generic professional filler. The post should sound specific to this platform and this topic, not swappable into any other real estate account's feed.
- **Pattern Breaker:** read it like someone who scrolls past a hundred real-estate-agent posts a day. If the structure or opening line feels like a template ("Did you know...", "Here's the truth about...") rewrite it.
- **Read-Aloud Test:** picture Joe actually saying this out loud to a practice owner. Fix anything that sounds like a caption written for an algorithm instead of a sentence a person would say.
- **Artificial Sentence Check:** find the single most "written by AI"-sounding line in the draft and rewrite it specifically — don't assume a pass on the whole post means every line is fine.

**Do not** invent fake hesitation, fake personal anecdotes, fake typos, or other manufactured "imperfections" to seem more human — that produces fake texture, not real voice, and conflicts directly with this skill's own rule against inventing a hypothetical scenario (see Step 1 and Guardrails). The fix for sounding like AI is a sharper, more specific point of view — never fabricated color.

## Interview-first drafting — Joe's words are the clay (added Jul 31, 2026; Lieberman Content Machine capture, Joe's go on #112)

When the post starts from an IDEA (a topic, a spike, "write something about X") rather than from substance Joe already stated, INTERVIEW HIM FIRST — 3-5 short questions, one at a time (his interview-box rule), pulling for specifics: the story, the number, what he actually thinks, what a doctor said. His answers become the transcript, and **the draft may only use his words — reshaped, tightened, ordered, but his — except the hook and the close.** The writer's job is shaping the clay, never inventing it. Skip the interview when his message already carries the substance (he told the story in the ask) or for calendar batches built from the banked substance he already provided. Why: this mechanizes the standing no-invented-scenarios rule and kills AI-tell at the root — slop is an input problem, and the interview is where the input gets good. If his answers stay thin, say so and offer to park the idea rather than padding it.

## Best-of-N drafting + evaluator gate for POSTS (added Jul 20, 2026; scored bar added Jul 31 — #112)

For an ORIGINAL post (not a reply), don't hand Joe one draft to react to. Draft the post 3-4 different ways in parallel: different hook angles on the same idea (number-reframe, insider-reveal, story-open, contrarian take — see hooks-deep-reference-2026.md). Then GRADE all of them against the rubric before Joe sees anything: the writing-audit skill (AI-tell bans, brand-voice, the recurring tells in voice-samples, the "would Joe say it out loud" test) plus Joe's substance and actual opinions in Step 7. **The bar is numeric (added Jul 31): score each surviving draft 1-10 against that rubric. Below 9, revise and re-score — the bar doesn't move, the draft does — before anything reaches Joe. Cap it at 3 revision rounds; a draft still under 9 after 3 rounds gets handed over anyway WITH its score and what's still weak named, never silently.** Hand Joe the best one or two with the other angles as backup, and a one-line note on why the top pick won. He picks or blends; the grader did the first cut so Joe reacts to strong options, not a single guess. Same generate -> evaluate -> repair loop the reply engine uses, applied to posts. Replies stay single-draft (they need speed); this is posts only.
