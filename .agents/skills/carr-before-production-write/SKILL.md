### 4039b9b5

DOCTRINE WRITING IS MODEL-AGNOSTIC. Any qualified seat writes doctrine — Fable, Sol 5.6, Opus, or whatever comes next. The "single writer, the Fable design seat" ownership stamp on doctrine files is RETIRED as of 2026-08-14 on Joe's ruling.

WHY IT EXISTED AND WHY IT NO LONGER DOES. It was never a judgement about which model was good enough. It was a FILE-ERA CONCURRENCY GUARD: doctrine lived in markdown on a merge-less medium, two writers could silently clobber each other, and "one seat edits this file" was the cheapest way to make a collision impossible — "a file with one writer cannot collide with itself." Doctrine now lives in the store. Postgres serializes every write, every section carries a version, and a genuine collision returns version_conflict as a question for a human instead of a silent overwrite. dna-protocol already stated the general principle: as each surface converts, the rule that protected it retires. This is that retirement for doctrine.

WHAT REPLACES IT, and it is stronger than the stamp was: base_version on every section write, the claim mechanism as a cooperative pre-warning, and the validation gates that run as hard preconditions on the body. None of those care which model is holding the pen.

WHAT IS NOT CHANGED BY THIS, and must not be conflated with it. The humanOnly boundary stands for every machine credential regardless of model: teach, retire-rule, confirm-merge, new-deal and reassign-deal refuse when human is false. That line is bearer-token-versus-human, not Fable-versus-Sol — a Fable session on a machine token is refused exactly the same verbs. A credential sitting in a file on a Mac must not be able to teach a rule that binds both partners. Anyone citing this rule to argue for widening a machine token has misread it.

Joe's framing was that the gate is obsolete "if Sol 5.6 and Fable are equal, model-agnostic seats". The conclusion is right and the reason is stronger than the premise: the gate would be obsolete even if the models were unequal, because what made it necessary was the medium, and the medium changed.

When open-source research on a contact (vendor, client, lead, party) finds a field that is wrong or blank, the session WRITES the corrected value to the record itself, through the owning verb, in the same run, and records the finding beside it with its source. THE DECISION PROCEDURE, in order: (1) Is the identity confirmed at high or medium confidence, with a second corroborating field (firm, email domain, city)? If not, do not apply: a near-match is still contamination, and when two candidates are plausible the session records both and picks neither. (2) Is the corrected value one clean value from a re-verifiable source? If it carries "unconfirmed" or "confirm", do not apply. Before overwriting, capture the prior value in the finding so the change is reversible; a rename of a shared org row still follows 8cddc6ad (count attachments, mint and re-point when shared). ### 7105955b

A RENUMBERED OR SUPERSEDED ROW IS NOT AN ABANDONED ONE, AND THE CLOSE MUST SAY SO IN ITS FIRST WORDS. The loop verbs offer only two resolutions, `done` and `dropped`, and neither fits a row whose work is continuing somewhere else — `done` is false because the work is not finished, and `dropped` is the one that inflates every abandonment measure built on this data. When a row is closed for a bookkeeping reason (a renumber, a merge into another loop, a split), use `dropped`, open the outcome with the fact that it was renumbered or superseded rather than abandoned, name the row that carries the work forward, and say plainly that anything measuring abandonment should exclude it. Never let a bookkeeping close read as a decision to stop doing something. The same discipline applies to any other verb whose resolution vocabulary is narrower than the situations it must record: state the real reason in the free-text field rather than letting the enum speak for you.

### c20dc3d5

THE ESCALATION CONTRACT — three constraints, each binding at a named moment, making an agent hierarchy safe rather than merely tidy. A pyramid prevents unowned scope; it does not prevent propagated error. MOMENT ONE — handing a finding upward, it carries its evidence: the exact query/command run, file and line, row count, tool call — never the conclusion alone. MOMENT TWO — receiving a subagent's finding and about to act on it or pass it up: RE-RUN IT FIRST if it is material (material = acting on it would WRITE, SPEND, SEND, or CHANGE A DECISION). Re-running means independently reproducing it from source, not evaluating the report; the seat that spawned the subagent owns this. You are answerable for whether you re-ran, not for whether you agreed. MOMENT THREE — about to act but missing something needed: go back down before guessing or interrupting a human. Consult the seat that produced the finding — it still holds the raw evidence. Escalate to a human only after the producing seat has been asked and could not resolve it.

### c8ebeb5b

A PRACTICE OWNER SELLING TO A PLATFORM IS A LEAD, AND THE LEAD IS THEIR NEXT STARTUP — NOT THE SALE. When a finding lane surfaces a practice being acquired (a DSO or platform filing an entity on an existing practice's address, an ownership change, a retirement sale), the exiting owner GOES ON THE LEAD LIST. The reason is never sell-side representation: CARR represents buyers and tenants only and does not sell practices, so there is no engagement in the exit itself and nobody should pitch one. The reason is the horizon. A doctor who sells to a platform commonly opens a new practice within a few years, and that startup IS a tenant or buyer representation. File them at a multi-year nurture cadence, never an outreach queue, and record WHY they are on the list so a later session does not mistake the row for a sell-side opportunity and pitch the wrong thing. Corollary: capture whether the seller owned or leased their building, because an owner exiting with a building and a tenant exiting a lease are different future conversations. Corollary: the acquiring platform is tracked as a national account rather than a lead — it is a counterparty to watch and a possible future tenant or buyer as it opens or relocates territory locations.

### faf1b643

Vendor relationship levels are defined by countable events, never by impression. 0 Prospective: identified, no two-way contact. 1 Building: the first TWO-WAY contact has happened — they replied, or you met; an outbound attempt alone does NOT promote anyone, because you can email fifty people and have fifty relationships that do not exist. 2 Established: value has moved in EITHER direction at least once — an introduction, a referral, or a deal. 3 Core: reciprocal AND repeated — value both ways, more than once. The level stays a human judgment and is stored, not computed: a relationship can matter for reasons no event count can see. But v_vendor_level_suggestion reports where the evidence disagrees with the recorded level, and a disagreement is worth a look in either direction — a Core vendor with nothing passing between you for months, or a Building vendor who has quietly sent three referrals.
