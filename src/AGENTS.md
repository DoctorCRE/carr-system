Joe works entire days inside these surfaces, so being stimulating and interesting to look at is a functional requirement of a workstation he lives in, not decoration.

THE DEFAULT IS AMBITIOUS, NOT RESTRAINED. Joe has granted standing permission to go big on motion, interactive elements and special effects. A session that trims an effect for being "too much" has misread him; the correct instinct is to build the richer version. Under-building is the likelier failure and it is the one to guard against.

What counts, and the list is a floor rather than a menu: an orchestrated entrance when a view loads, state changes that animate between before and after instead of cutting, ambient life in the background so the surface looks switched on, diagram elements that draw and flow so the eye follows the movement, hover and press feedback on everything interactive, values that count up when data lands, scroll-triggered reveals, and depth responses such as parallax or tilt that follow the cursor. Build more in that family.

INTERACTIVE BEATS DECORATIVE where both are available. A diagram whose nodes respond to the cursor and cross-highlight related rows elsewhere on the page is worth more than the same diagram with an animation played over it.

TWO HARD LIMITS, unchanged and not subject to taste. First, `prefers-reduced-motion: reduce` is honored on every surface with a static fallback that keeps all content visible and all state legible, because vestibular disorders are real and this is an accessibility floor. Verify it by forcing the fallback and measuring, never by reading the CSS. Second, motion never delays or obstructs: nothing a partner needs to read or click waits on an animation, no entrance sequence runs beyond about a second, ambient motion never shifts layout or pulls the eye off something being read, and effects must hold frame rate on a laptop.

THE TEST, before a surface is called done: does it look switched on and rewarding to sit in front of for eight hours, or does it look like a screenshot of itself?

AUDIT SIGNAL: a surface shipped or reviewed without ambient life and interaction, or with motion that ignores the reduced-motion setting, is named out loud in that session's report as non-compliant, with what should have moved.

### eeb3d106

CONSTRAINT, binding at the moment a session BUILDS OR REVIEWS ANY CARR WORKSTATION SURFACE - the command center, a board, the Deal Room, any live dashboard: the surface must FEEL ALIVE, and RATE OF MOTION IS A STATE CHANNEL. Joe's mechanic: a calm slow pulse on things that are working, a faster pulse on things that need attention.

THE SCALE, so every surface encodes state the same way and a partner learns it once: healthy or progressing pulses slowly, roughly a three to four second breath. Something wanting attention pulses at about two seconds. Something overdue or blocking pulses fastest, near one second. Dormant, parked or closed does not pulse at all, and stillness therefore means nothing is asking for you. Faster always means more urgent, never the reverse.

Pulse rate is a REDUNDANT channel layered on top of color and shape, never a replacement for them, so the surface still reads correctly for a partner using color assist or a still screenshot.

ALIVE ALSO MEANS RESPONSIVE: anything interactive answers the cursor, live data announces itself when it changes rather than silently re-rendering, and presence of the other partner is visible rather than implied.

HARD LIMIT: honor `prefers-reduced-motion: reduce` with a static fallback that keeps the state legible through color and shape alone, because a pulsing interface is genuinely harmful to some people. Ambient pulse must never move layout or pull the eye off something being read.

THE TEST, before a workstation surface is called done: does it look switched on, or does it look like a screenshot of itself?

AUDIT SIGNAL: a workstation surface shipped or reviewed with no ambient life, or with pulse rates that do not follow the scale above, is named out loud in that session's report as non-compliant with what should have been alive.
