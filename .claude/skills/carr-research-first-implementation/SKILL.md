### 2e362970

EVERY NEW IMPLEMENTATION STARTS WITH A DEEP-DIVE RESEARCH PASS, BEFORE ANY BUILDING. Gather the available guidance, expert practice and current documentation on the topic first, then design, then build. The research is a phase of the work, not preparation for it, and it is not optional because the schedule is tight.

WHAT TRIGGERS IT: standing up a new capability, adopting a tool, configuring a platform the partner does not know well, or building a workflow for the first time. NOT every task — a bug fix, a routine edit, or another instance of something already understood does not need it. The test is whether the work is the FIRST of its kind here.

WHY, in Joe's own framing when he asked for it on the Outlook session: "i don't know what i dont know." Poking at a UI surfaces only what is visible. Building from memory surfaces only what the session already believed. Neither finds the feature, the setting or the established practice that would have changed the design — and the cost of missing it is not a worse build, it is a build that is quietly worse in a way nobody notices for months.

WHAT THE PASS MUST ACTUALLY COVER, so this does not decay into one search: the current feature surface including anything shipped recently, guidance from people who genuinely operate at this volume rather than vendor marketing, what the partner ALREADY OWNS and is under-using (paid capability sitting idle is the most common finding), and the known failure modes others have hit. Cite live sources; a claim without one does not survive the pass.

THE COST OF SKIPPING IT, recorded because it is not hypothetical. On 2026-08-03 a session spent hours attacking Outlook's scripting model, its local store and its proprietary binary format to read Joe's mail, including a security review and two patches to a third-party parser. The actual answer was to add the same Exchange account to Apple Mail, which took him two minutes and gave 202 mailboxes, full history and faithful bodies. No research pass was run at the start. The session anchored on "get data out of Outlook" instead of "read Joe's mail", and never asked what other clients could hold the account.

HOW IT COMPOSES WITH THE RULES ALREADY IN FORCE: it runs BEFORE the build-our-own default, because pricing an in-house build against alternatives requires knowing what the alternatives are. It also runs before adopting third-party code, where Joe's sequence is search, then examine for dangerous code, then build. Research first is the general case; those are its specific applications.
