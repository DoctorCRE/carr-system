### cd84d22a

DECLINE THE CLAUDE CODE FEEDBACK-SURVEY TRANSCRIPT SHARE FROM ANY CARR SESSION.

Never consent to the Claude Code feedback-survey transcript share from a CARR session, and tell the partner to decline whenever the survey appears.

WHAT CHANGED. Claude Code 2.1.224 extended that share. It no longer sends only the conversation: with the human's consent it also uploads the last request's model settings, the TOOL DEFINITIONS, the model parameters, and the SYSTEM PROMPT. The system prompt is the problem. It carries CLAUDE.md verbatim, which means the standing project instructions, the partner names, the territory, the positioning, the vault's pointer structure, and the taught-rule index. Secrets are redacted and oversized fields are dropped first, but neither of those protects any of the above.

WHY IT BINDS HERE. Any CARR session may be holding client-confidential material at the moment the survey appears, and a session working a live deal is exactly the session most likely to have produced a survey-worthy experience. The publication firewall is absolute: internal-only material never publishes, client confidentiality is absolute, and when in doubt, hold. Consenting to this share is a publication event.

WHAT BINDS, AT THE MOMENT THE SURVEY IS OFFERED.
1. The session never encourages, suggests, or assists the share.
2. If a session observes the survey being offered, it says plainly: decline the transcript share, it now uploads CLAUDE.md and the tool definitions.
3. This holds for Joe's sessions and Dell's sessions alike, on every surface, with no exception for "the session looked harmless." The person deciding cannot see what is in the system prompt; the rule exists so they do not have to check.

WHY IT IS A TAUGHT RULE AND NOT A SETTING. There is no configuration flag that disables this share. It is consent-gated, so it cannot fire on its own, which also means the ONLY control is the human's answer in the moment. A rule that recites at session start is the only channel that reaches that moment.

### f2d58a30

EVERY CLIENT-FACING RECOMMENDATION NAMES AT LEAST ONE THING WE WOULD NOT DO, AND WHY. One honest limit per option, stated in the document rather than saved for the meeting. Applies to option sets, search packets, proposals, tour shortlists, LOI recommendations, the Deal Council's empty-chair close, and any public page. WHY IT IS SAFE FOR CARR SPECIFICALLY AND FOR ALMOST NOBODY ELSE: a broker who represents landlords cannot tell a client to skip a space without costing their own listing. CARR never represents landlords or sellers, so saying "I would not take this one, and here is why" costs nothing and is the conflict-of-interest argument DEMONSTRATED instead of asserted. It converts the pitch from a claim about our incentives into evidence of them.
