## The craft that works (distilled from studied voices; refresh as new reads teach more)
- **Cold-open that cliffs before the fold.** Open inside a real situation (a story or a sharp claim) and put the natural cliff right before X's "Show more" cut, so the reader taps in. First line written last, judged hardest (voice-profile §8.2).
- **Explain through consequences, not definitions.** Teach a technical concept (NNN, TI allowance, escalators) by showing what goes wrong, not what it means. (Kagan explains NNN via "a KFC turned into a pot dispensary," never a textbook definition.)
- **One sticky, concrete image per concept.** A vivid specific picture beats abstract description and is what gets bookmarked ("your house behind a building you've outgrown," not "the personal guaranty carries risk").
- **Sharp rhythm.** Short punchy sentences cutting through the longer explanatory ones (Kagan: "Sounds amazing, right? Well, there are downsides." Huber: staccato builds). Uneven pacing = the humanity-pass thought-flow rule in action.
- **Argue against yourself.** Concede when leasing is right, when representation isn't needed. The advocate who occasionally argues against his own fee is believed on everything else (voice-profile §8.5; Huber: "You can win. But the odds are against you.").
- **Blunt-stake ending, no tidy recap.** End on a stake or a jab, never a corporate summary bow. One low-key CTA is fine ("my inbox is open"); no hard sell.
- **Specific or silent.** Real numbers stated plainly, no source tags (voice-profile §8.1).
- **Length:** ~1,000 words is the sweet spot for an Article.

So deliver the final review/publish copy as a FORMATTED **.docx** (opens directly in Google Docs, renders headers + bold, and pastes into the composer with formatting intact). ## Distribution — how to PROMOTE a published Article (added 2026-07-16; verified vs 2026 X mechanics)
An X Article is NATIVE to X, so promoting it carries **NO external-link penalty** (off-site links lose 50-90% reach; the Article lives on X). The 2026 algorithm favors long-form (it pulls 40-60% more impressions than threads) and X is actively surfacing Articles (the $1M best-article prize), so the Article gets a tailwind — the job is to point followers at it well.
- **The launch/companion post is a HOOK, not an announcement.** Never "just published a new article 👇". Lead with the single sharpest moment from the piece (first line is the whole game, voice-profile §8.2). Best mechanic: QUOTE-POST the Article with the hook (re-surfaces it, no link penalty); or post the hook and drop the Article in a reply. Hook angles that worked for the medical-lease piece: the "you sign 1-2 leases, they sign hundreds" gap (authority, recommended), the dentist cold-open (story), the 60-second occupancy-cost test (bookmark bait).
- **Be present the first 30-60 minutes.** Reply to every early comment yourself — first-hour engagement sets the reach ceiling and author-engaged replies are the most valuable signal on X now (the reply engine).
- **Pin the launch post** as the authority piece new profile visitors land on.
- **Repurpose ONE Article into a week of standalone posts.** Each section is a self-contained post (the occupancy-cost check, the personal guaranty, renewal leverage, the DSO real-estate math). Drip them as standalone posts over the following days (no link needed — they stand alone and pull bookmarks); a couple can point back to the full Article. One Article feeds a week of feed.
- **Feed the reply engine.** When a lease / buy-vs-lease / occupancy question comes up in a target account's thread, the Article is the deep answer to drop in.
- Cadence: 1-2 Articles/month; publish and promote when the audience is active. Sources: Sprout Social + Social Media Today, 2026.

## Lessons banked from Joe's reviews (apply to EVERY future article)
- **Get Joe's actual role right.** Joe works the LEASE (negotiation + review), not the build-out. The build-out is the NEXT phase, run by the GC, architect, and equipment supplier; Joe is the one who makes those introductions and brings the vendors in. Never write a scenario where Joe is asked for build-out help. His real high-value moment: reviewing/negotiating a lease BEFORE the client signs. (Medical-lease article rev 1→2, Jul 16 — the cloud draft had a dentist ask Joe for build-out help after signing; wrong on both counts.)
- **A referral often comes through a vendor.** A realistic origin story: the client heard what Joe does from their equipment rep (or lender/CPA), then asked him to look over the deal. Shows the network without stating it.
- **Leases are long.** A commercial healthcare lease is typically 20+ pages, not 5. Don't undersell the document.
- **Anchor with real numbers.** Don't just cite a healthy range; give a concrete worked example (e.g., $850K collections / $72K base rent = 8.5% occupancy). Specific-or-silent (voice-profile §8.1).
