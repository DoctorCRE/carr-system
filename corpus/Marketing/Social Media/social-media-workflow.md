## Format rotation is MANDATORY every batch (Joe, July 24, 2026 — do not ship all-single-cards)

We spent real time building per-platform format doctrine; deploy it. A batch that ships one single-image card per post is a miss, even if the copy is good. The single stat card is ONE format, reserved for when a single number is the whole point — it is not the default. Every weekly batch must rotate formats and lean on each platform's proven winners:

- **LinkedIn:** the document/multi-slide **carousel is the hero format** (highest dwell + engagement) — aim for at least one carousel most weeks; **native text posts** (no image) are the workhorse and a legitimate distinct format; single cards are the exception. Polls occasionally. (linkedin-strategy-2026.md format doctrine.)
- **Facebook:** rotate **carousels** (multi-point education), original single graphics, and the **text-question post** (Mari Smith plain-text-on-color question) that pulls comments. Reels when the video lane is off hold. (facebook-strategy-2026.md.)
- **Instagram:** rotate **carousels** (how-to / multi-point, swipe-through), single stat cards, and reels when off hold. (instagram-strategy-2026.md.)
- **X:** standalone-first, but rotate in **story-time long-form single posts**, the occasional **thread** (via additionalPosts), the recurring-series slot, and **article-repurpose** posts. (x-strategy-2026.md traction regroup.)

Carousels render on the same local headless-Chrome card pipeline: build numbered slides (hook cover → one point per slide → takeaway/CTA close), a page indicator and a "swipe" cue on the cover, then pass the slide URLs as an ordered `mediaUrls` array to `blotato_create_post` (LinkedIn and Instagram both accept multi-image carousels; Facebook takes multi-image too). Vision-check every slide. Log the FORMAT of each post in the batch file and the performance log so the monthly review can steer format, not just topic.

**Self-check before a batch is called done:** across the week's posts, are at least 3 distinct formats deployed, with a carousel on at least one CARR platform and no platform running all single cards? If not, rebuild before scheduling.

### Animated statics — a STATIC format with motion, not a video-lane asset (Joe, July 25, 2026)

A card we authored, rebuilt as a ~6-second stop-motion build: elements jump into place pose to pose, locked camera, no narration, and the final second holds on the finished static so the last frame IS the card. Built locally: `~/carr-system/video/make-animated-static.sh <layers-folder> <name>` (runtime `~/Movies/CARR Video Pipeline`; outputs an MP4 for the feed and a GIF). Input is one full-canvas transparent PNG per element, numbered in stacking order — we author the statics, so the layers already exist.

**Classification (Joe's call, the load-bearing decision):** this competes with carousels and single cards for a FORMAT slot. It does **not** draw against the video lane's ~1/week allocation. That allocation is for Joe on camera, which is the thing that builds trust and cannot be faked, and an animated card must never crowd it out. It also does not fall under the reels quality hold: it is motion graphics of our own designed card, not synthesized footage pretending to be real, so it carries none of the tells that put that hold in place.

**Dose — two per month across all CARR platforms, never in consecutive weeks, at most one per batch.** Roughly 5% of the monthly CARR-platform slots. Deliberate minority, same posture as video.

**It has to earn the slot.** The build must add meaning, not just motion — a stack of numbers assembling, a comparison forming, a sequence where the order is the point. **If the card would read identically as a still, post the still.** That constraint keeps the volume honest better than the quota does.

**Placement:** Instagram and Facebook are the homes. **Never X** (the no-video-on-X rule stands, and quote-tweets are the priority there). LinkedIn rarely, because the document carousel is the hero format there and this would compete for the same slot.

**Never the same shape twice.** The builder refuses to repeat a recent choreography: `plan-animated-static.py` picks the build order, entrance directions, cadence, duration and landing sound against the choreography log and excludes whatever ran lately. The log lives where the builder writes it — `~/Movies/CARR Video Pipeline/choreography-log.tsv`, reachable from the repo as `~/carr-system/video/choreography-log.tsv` (symlink, set 2026-08-23). Read THAT file before deciding animated statics are blocked: the 8/10 and 8/17 batches both skipped the format after inferring a wrong path, while two real builds sat logged since 7/25. Do not pin `--concept` just to get a familiar look. Seven concepts, five landing sounds; one sound per piece, varying between pieces only (the CRITIQUE-LOG v3 rule).

**The trap, named in advance.** These publish as video on IG and Facebook and will likely catch the same format boost that gave the July reel ~10x reach over static. That reach came from Instagram pushing a FORMAT, not from the piece being good. Reach is not quality. Do not let a strong first number argue this up from occasional to weekly — that is exactly how the format burns out, and the whole advantage here is novelty.

**Log it as its own Format value: `animated card`.** We have zero performance data on this format for Joe, which is the honest reason the dose starts small. The monthly review on the 15th steers it from the Format column, not from instinct.

**The talking-head production system (deliberately small, because over-producing reintroduces the fake feeling):**

**Retention is the SCRIPT, not the production (added July 31, 2026 — craft salvaged from a faceless-YouTube playbook whose model itself is exactly what this lane's redirect rejected; his one credible datum: fixing the script alone took the same channel from 200 to 40,000 views).** The platform measures one thing — do people keep watching — and the script decides it before the camera turns on. The arc, compressed for this lane's 15-45s clips: **cold open on the number or counterintuitive claim (no intro, no "hey guys" — the existing 3-second rule, sharpened: open on the CLAIM, not on context) → make them FEEL it in one beat ("you" + a specific number) → the single takeaway → a close that calls back to the open.** For anything longer (60s+, or if a YouTube lane ever opens): add a credibility line under 30 seconds ("I negotiate these leases every week"), a pattern interrupt — cut, b-roll, tone shift — every ~90 seconds, and a close that earns the follow rather than begging it. ## Peer-gap pass — the whitespace sourcing step (added July 18, 2026)

Companion to the trend & news pass, and a different question. Trend & news asks "what is happening right now." The peer-gap pass asks **"what are the other voices in this niche all saying, and what are they all avoiding — so we claim the whitespace."** Idea adapted from a LinkedIn-growth thread (Logan Gott, Jul 13, 2026), stripped of its X-only argument-baiting. Run it **periodically, not every batch** (roughly monthly, or when the content feels same-y), since the peer landscape moves slower than the news.

**The method:**
1. **Map the field with Grok (the better tool than clicking profiles one by one).** Grok has live X access and can synthesize across many accounts at once. Ask it: who are the prominent voices in CRE, healthcare CRE, MOB investment, and practice real estate; what topics/angles do they cover HEAVILY (the crowded lanes); and what angles do they systematically UNDER-serve or dodge that an independent practice owner actually wants answered. The reputable CRE voices publish mostly on LinkedIn, so treat Grok's X view as a proxy and, when a specific competitor matters, read their actual LinkedIn/X posts in Joe's Chrome to ground-truth it.
2. **Verify, don't relay.** Grok's synthesis is the starting map. Spot-check by reading a couple of real posts before trusting a "they all avoid X" claim — same discipline as the reply engine.
3. **Convert gaps into ownable angles.** The output is a short list of whitespace angles Joe can own: things his ICP wants, that his tenant/buyer-side vantage uniquely lets him say, that the crowded field skips. These feed the calendar alongside trend & news.

**LinkedIn register guardrail (hard):** the source thread ranked angles "by how likely to start an argument." That is X-register and WRONG for the CARR platforms. LinkedIn is Joe's measured, vendor-and-referral-facing account, and it includes listing brokers he actively does deals with (voice-profile §3). So take the gap-FINDING and drop the provocation: claim the whitespace as generous, educational insight, never as a fight. Model contrast only, never impugn a counterpart. Everything else holds — writing-rules bans, HIPAA, publication firewall, plain-number rule.

**Output goes to** a dated peer-gap note in this folder + the whitespace angles are added to `content-inspiration-bank.md` (substance) so the calendar can pull them. Log the run in the inspiration-bank feedback log.
