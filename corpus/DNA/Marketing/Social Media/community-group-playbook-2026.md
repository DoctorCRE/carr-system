## The one thing that governs everything: the purpose

Before rules or growth comes the purpose (Priya Parker's first principle of gathering). This group exists to be a protected regional space where healthcare practice OWNERS and operators help each other run and own a practice, real estate included but not limited to it, without being pitched by vendors. That is the whole promise. It is a peer community, not a CARR channel. Every later call (who gets in, what gets posted, what gets removed) serves that one purpose, and when a call is unclear, the purpose decides it.

The value to a doctor is scarcity. They are pitched constantly, and a clean room of peers who actually operate practices in their own market is something they cannot get anywhere else. That scarcity is the entire draw.

## Positioning: neutral community, transparent hosting (Joe's call)

- The group is ABOUT practice owners, not about CARR. The name, the About, and the content are all community-first.
- Hosting is TRANSPARENT, not hidden. The About and the welcome state plainly that Joe and Dell are healthcare real estate agents at CARR who host and moderate the group, and that it is deliberately not a marketing channel for their business. Naming the affiliation openly is what makes it trustworthy; hiding it would poison the trust the moment anyone found out.
- The reward for hosting is being the known, trusted name, never a pitch inside the room (see "The business link" below).

**Name (LOCKED July 18, 2026): Practice Owners of the Panhandle & South AL.** Chosen for three reasons: it leads with the IDENTITY ("Practice Owners") so the mobile group-header truncates the region tail, not the important half; "Panhandle" reaches east to Tallahassee and "South AL" covers Mobile and Dothan, so the whole territory is named; and the abbreviation keeps it short. No "Healthcare" in the name (Joe, Jul 18): every member is invited and vetted, so they already know it is a healthcare-practice room; the word would only help cold Facebook search, which this private, warm-first group deliberately does not rely on, and dropping it keeps the name short for mobile.

**Naming principle (learned from the prior name).** The name must REPEL vendors and CENTER owners. The former name, "Gulf Coast Healthcare Business Network" with the tagline "Connecting Health with Financial Success," did the opposite: "business network" and "financial success" read transactional and were a magnet for the exact lenders, CPAs, and consultants the group exists to keep out, and they never told an owner "this is for you." Owner-centered wording only; no "business / network / financial" framing.

**Banner and visual identity (mobile-first).** Neutral teal-navy, deliberately NOT CARR's navy-and-orange, since this is a neutral community. Mobile-first rules: center everything in the safe zone (mobile crops the sides and shrinks to a thumbnail), keep it bold and legible, and treat the banner as ATMOSPHERE, because the Facebook group NAME carries the identity on mobile, not text baked into the banner. The hero visual is an accurate map of Florida and Alabama with the TERRITORY highlighted in teal (southern AL plus the FL panhandle west of Tallahassee), the peninsula and north Alabama left muted. Real cartography, never hand-drawn: the FL and AL state paths come from a real dataset, clipped to the territory band. No rising growth-arrow (which read "financial success," the framing we dropped). The tagline "Owners only · no vendors, no pitches" doubles as positioning. Current banner: `DNA/Marketing/Social Media/community-banner-practice-owners-2026-07-18.png`.

## The field's playbook: what the best community builders teach, applied

- **Priya Parker (The Art of Gathering)** — purpose first, and generous EXCLUSION. Who you keep out defines the room as much as who you let in. A vendor-free, owner-only space is a deliberate act of hosting, and the host has both the right and the duty to protect the gathering.
- **Charles Vogl (The Art of Community)** — belonging is built with a clear BOUNDARY (who is inside), a threshold (the vetting to cross it), shared VALUES, insider language, and RITUALS (recurring things members can count on). Design these on purpose, not by accident.
- **David Spinks (CMX, the SPACES model)** — communities succeed on Support, Product, Acquisition, Contribution, Engagement, Success. For this group the engine is peer SUPPORT and member CONTRIBUTION. Members helping members is the product, not anything CARR posts.
- **Richard Millington (FeverBee)** — active communities run on habit and recurring rhythm, and on content that provokes CONVERSATION rather than applause. Measure participation, not size.
- **Seth Godin (Tribes)** — smallest viable audience, "people like us." Independent practice owners in this region are exactly a "people like us" identity; lean into it.

## Membership and vetting (the gate that makes the no-spam promise real)

Private group, request to join, every member vetted. This gate is the single most important mechanism, because the promise is only as real as the door.

**Who is IN:** owners and operators of independent healthcare practices in the territory (dental, medical, veterinary, optometry, chiropractic, physical therapy, med spa, wellness, and the rest), plus associate providers on an ownership track. Local, all verticals.

**Who is OUT:** vendors and anyone who sells to practices (lenders, CPAs, attorneys, contractors, architects, equipment and supply reps, marketers, recruiters, consultants, software), non-local accounts, and anyone whose reason to join is to reach the doctors. This is the whole point, and it is enforced at the door, not after the spam lands.

**Membership questions (the vetting), required on every join request:**
1. What practice do you own or work in, and in what city?
2. What is your role? (owner / associate / practice manager / other)
3. Do you sell any product or service to healthcare practices? (a plain honesty check; a yes is a polite decline)

Answers get checked against the profile. No answers, no entry.

**Anti-spam layers (Admin Assist).** Three layers stack to roughly an 85 to 95% spam reduction: (1) the membership questions above; (2) auto-decline join requests from profiles created in the last 90 days or with no profile photo; (3) keyword auto-filters that hold posts with sales or pitch language for review. Plus a watch list and a three-strikes-then-remove rule for anyone who slips through and starts selling.

## The rules (pinned, plain, enforced consistently)

Short, plain, pinned to the top and in the About. Consistency of enforcement matters more than the exact wording, because inconsistent moderation reads as unfair and erodes the trust that is the whole asset.
1. This is a room for practice owners and operators. No selling, no pitching, no promotion, no DMs offering services. Vendors are not admitted.
2. Real questions, real help, real experience. Be useful.
3. What is shared here stays here. No screenshots out, nothing patient-identifying (HIPAA applies to all of us), no client-identifying detail.
4. Disagree well. No attacks.
5. Your hosts are CARR agents who keep the room clean. They will not pitch you here, and they ask that no one else does either.

## The content and engagement engine

Members helping members is the product; the hosts prime the pump and hold the rhythm. Conversation is the goal, so posts are QUESTIONS and prompts far more than broadcasts (a post with 40 comments outruns one with 200 likes in the group algorithm).

**Four recurring pillars (rituals members can count on):**
- **Problem-solving:** "post your problem, the room weighs in" — a lease clause, a hiring headache, a bad vendor experience, an equipment decision.
- **Teach / authority:** a plain, useful lesson from a host or a member. This is where the lease and market knowledge fits, given as help, never as a CARR pitch.
- **Conversation / experience:** a simple, specific, debatable question ("what is the most overrated advice you got when you opened your practice?").
- **Member spotlight / wins:** a member's win, a new location, an anniversary. Makes people feel seen and pulls contribution.

**A starter weekly rhythm** (then tune to what the room responds to):
- Monday: a conversation question (easy, high participation).
- Wednesday: a teach or problem-solving post.
- Friday: a member spotlight or a lighter prompt.

Claude drafts these weekly from the substance bank and the card system; Joe or Dell posts, and, crucially, replies fast to keep the thread alive (early replies drive the group's reach). The register inside the group is peer-host: warm, low-key, human, never a marketing voice.

**Seed BEFORE launch.** A new member who joins to a sparse feed leaves immediately. Before inviting anyone, seed 20 to 30 real posts across the four pillars so the room looks alive on day one. This is the first build task.

## Cold start and growth

The hard part is the first 30 to 50 engaged members and steady activity; most groups die empty. Sequence:
1. **Seed the content** (above), so the room is not empty.
2. **Invite warm first.** Joe's and Dell's own networks, current and past clients and prospects, and the lead-magnet opt-ins. Warm people who already trust the hosts convert to members and actually participate. This is the highest-leverage channel by far.
3. **Then feed it from the other surfaces.** A soft mention on the CARR platforms and in the newsletter once it is live, and the lead-magnet follow-up can invite qualified owners. Instagram overlaps well with this audience.
4. **Adjacent cross-promo later:** admins of non-competing local professional or small-business groups, a genuine mutual recommendation.

The first milestone is active participation, not a member count. 30 to 50 owners who post and comment beats 500 who lurk.

## The business link (how a value-first community becomes business with no pitch)

This is the discipline that makes or breaks it. The group produces business ONLY by making Joe and Dell the trusted, top-of-mind names, never by pitching inside it. The mechanism:
- Trust and familiarity compound in a room where the hosts consistently help and never sell. When a member eventually has a lease event or a purchase, the host is the obvious call.
- The group is also a standing warm audience, a live content source (real member questions become content everywhere else), and a referral network (members refer each other, and members refer the hosts).
- In Customer Value Journey terms, the group is the community layer under Engage through Advocate: it nurtures, retains, and turns members into referrers.

**The hard rule:** the hosts do not pitch in the group, ever. The reward is the trust; the transaction happens off to the side, when the member reaches out. A host who pitches kills the room. If a member asks a real estate question, answer it as genuine help and take any actual business to DM or offline, never as a group broadcast.

## Moderation operations

- **Vetting:** review every join request against the membership questions and the profile; decline vendors politely.
- **Admin Assist:** configure the three anti-spam layers above.
- **Daily-ish presence:** review pending posts, scan the feed, reply in threads while they are young.
- **Enforcement:** consistent, per the pinned rules; a watch list for borderline members; three strikes then remove for anyone who starts selling.
- **Host back-channel:** Joe and Dell keep a private thread to decide questionable calls together and stay consistent.

## Measurement

Judge the group on PARTICIPATION, not size: the percent of members who post or comment, comments per post, and returning contributors, plus the qualitative read (are real owners getting real help). Member count is a vanity number; an active room of 100 beats a dead one of 1,000. Review monthly with the rest of the performance loop.
