## What Instagram is for (the goal)

Instagram is the discovery and reach platform. Unlike Facebook, which builds local relationships, Instagram can put CARR content in front of people who do not follow the account yet, because it ranks by interest, not just who you know. It is visual-first and it rewards content worth forwarding. For CARR that means reaching practice owners and their networks with save-worthy, share-worthy healthcare real estate content that travels beyond the current following.

The one action that matters most on Instagram in 2026 is the DM share, the send. When someone taps the paper-airplane and forwards a post to a friend, Instagram reads that as the strongest possible signal of genuine value and pushes the content to new audiences. So the guiding question for every Instagram post is: would a practice owner text this to a colleague who is about to sign a lease? Build for that, and reach follows. Success here is measured in sends, saves, reach to non-followers, and profile visits, not likes.

---

## What the 2026 Instagram algorithm rewards

Instagram ranks differently on each surface, and knowing which surface a post lives on tells you what to optimize.

**Reels (the discovery engine).** DM sends are the most heavily weighted signal for reaching new audiences, followed by watch time, then saves. The metadata that helps is a strong visual hook, trending or original audio, and text overlays. Viewers decide within roughly 1.7 seconds whether to keep watching, so the opening is everything. This is where new reach comes from.

**Feed (carousels punch above their weight).** Carousels outperform single images and Reels on engagement, averaging around 10% versus roughly 6 to 7% for the others, because each swipe is another chance to hold attention and earn a save. The feed weighs saves, shares, comments, and profile taps, plus how likely someone is to stop and spend a few seconds.

**Explore (pure discovery).** Ranked by engagement velocity (early likes, comments, shares, saves) and by content people want to forward. Trending audio and strong hooks help a post surface here to people who have never seen the account.

**Stories (hold the existing audience).** Stories rarely reach new people; they deepen the relationship with current followers through interactive stickers (polls, questions, add-yours) and DMs. Use them to stay present and start conversations, not to grow.

Two rules cut across all surfaces: Instagram rewards original content and demotes anything downloaded and reshared, and it penalizes other-app watermarks (a TikTok logo will cost you reach).

## Format doctrine (what to post, and why)

Instagram is a two-engine platform: Reels for discovery, carousels for engagement. Use both deliberately.

1. **Reels are the reach engine.** 30 to 90 seconds for educational content, 7 to 15 for something light. Native 9:16 (1080x1920), a hook in the first two seconds, jump cuts every 3 to 5 seconds (which lift engagement meaningfully), text overlays since about half watch muted, trending or original audio, and never a watermark from another app. Follow the video lane runbook (real footage default, every scene relevant, no AI tells, CARR overlay). Instagram is the primary home for CARR Reels.
2. **Carousels are the engagement and save engine, and they are underused.** 6 to 10 slides standard, and longer sequences can keep pulling reach out to around slide 13. The first slide has roughly a 24% exit rate, so slide one has to hook or nobody swipes. Build reference-style carousels people save and forward: "what a TI allowance really means," the five vendors around a deal, the lease-versus-buy math, due-diligence pitfalls. One idea per slide. Use 1080x1350 (4:5) or 1080x1080.
3. **Single photos** for a strong local or market image, used sparingly since they engage below carousels.
4. **Stories** daily-ish to hold the audience: interactive stickers, behind-the-scenes, reshares, questions. Relationship maintenance, not reach.

A high-performing Reel can be repurposed into a step-by-step carousel, and a strong carousel can seed a Reel. Reels find new people; carousels convert them into savers and followers.

## Caption SEO has replaced hashtags

This is the biggest change most people miss. Instagram removed hashtag-following at the end of 2024, and hashtags no longer drive algorithmic reach. Keywords do.

- **Front-load the words the audience searches** in the first one or two sentences and within the visible 125-character preview: healthcare real estate, practice owner, lease vs buy, dental office, and the market name where relevant.
- **Put keywords in the bio** too, since the bio is searchable and shapes what the account is known for.
- **Write custom alt text** with the target keywords on image posts, because Instagram uses it to categorize content.
- **Hashtags: 3 to 5 highly relevant ones, maximum.** They retain minor search value and no real distribution value. Do not stack 30.
- Captions often engage best kept tight, with a strong hook up top and the depth below.

## What to post (CARR pillars, Instagram-native)

The three pillars and the 40/30/30 mix still govern the topic split; Instagram changes the delivery. Direct-to-doctor tenant advice and the conflict-of-interest angle run well here (as on Facebook, unlike LinkedIn).

- **Educational / Market Authority (40%):** reference carousels and explainer Reels a practice owner would save and forward. This is Instagram's sweet spot, because it is both save-worthy and send-worthy.
- **Local Market Activity (30%):** visual, local, real. Market imagery, openings, submarket notes, delivered as Reels or photo posts with keyword captions.
- **Brand / Behind-the-Scenes (30%):** the mission, the team, the tenant-and-buyer-only model, in a warm visual register, largely through Stories and the occasional Reel.

Frame the save-worthy and send-worthy angle explicitly: the post is the thing a doctor texts to a colleague, or bookmarks before a lease negotiation.

## The field's playbook: what the best Instagram operators actually do (added July 18, 2026)

A research pass on the ten most influential voices in Instagram brand marketing, distilled to what transfers to a local healthcare-CRE account. The algorithm section above is WHAT the platform rewards; this is HOW the best operators earn it. Sources at the foot.

**The ten and the one thing to take from each:**
1. **Brock Johnson** (Reels growth) — the hook is everything. The first 2 to 3 seconds, or the first slide, decide reach. A face in the hook lifts clicks; open with a bold statement, a common mistake, a surprising tip, or a relatable problem. His 2026 call: repeatable SERIES and challenges, a named recurring format people come back for.
2. **Jasmine Star** — Attract / Nurture / Convert. Every post has one of three jobs: attract a stranger, nurture a follower, or convert. And "share who you are, not just what you do," because the human is what builds the relationship that converts. Batch-plan so the cadence never breaks.
3. **Vanessa Lau** — content PILLARS and the 3-step build: define the one persona, pick the pillars (Attract, Educate, Connect), generate topics that convert. Instagram reads your last 9 to 12 posts to categorize your niche, so pillar discipline is what makes the algorithm file you correctly.
4. **Sue B. Zimmerman** — use Instagram to become the go-to AUTHORITY in one industry. Own your value, stay visible, teach. Authority in a narrow lane beats broad reach.
5. **Rachel Karten** (Link in Bio) — two things. Your strategy needs TENSION, a real point of view rather than safe brand-asset wallpaper. And do NOT show up the same way on every platform, which is "detrimental to brand health." Instagram is not a mirror of Facebook. A platform strategy can be doing ONE format really well.
6. **Elise Darma** — Reels for business without dancing, plus Instagram SEO. Put the keywords the audience searches into the caption and profile. Teach through short useful Reels and story-led selling.
7. **Alex Tooby** — Instagram SEO and caption craft. Discovery now runs through search and keywords, not hashtag dumps. Write captions that get found and that pull a response.
8. **Amy Porterfield** — educate first, nurture toward a next step. Lead with value that stands alone.
9. **Gary Vaynerchuk** — document, do not create; native content; give value many times before an ask. Volume of genuine value, made for the platform.
10. **Mari Smith** (Meta authority) — short-form video is the reach engine; judge posts by feedback percentage over raw likes; reply fast to build the conversation the algorithm rewards.

**What this changes about how an Instagram post is BUILT here (the upgrade):**
- **The unit of reach is the SEND.** DM shares carry the most weight for reaching non-followers on Instagram, more than on Facebook. Build posts a practice owner wants to send to their partner, spouse, or associate ("show this to your dentist friend before they sign"). Relatable takes, useful checklists, and surprising data are the most-sent shapes.
- **Carousels are the power format here.** They have the highest engagement of any format, every swipe counts, and the algorithm re-shows them to people who did not finish. This is where the shared CARD SYSTEM does its best work: one archetype per slide, 6 to 10 slides, one lesson each, a hook cover, and a "send this" or "save this" close. The card lookbook archetypes ARE the carousel slides.
- **The cover slide is the hook.** Same hook craft as everywhere (callout plus curiosity-or-promise plus a concrete detail), tuned warm for Instagram, on the first slide or the first line. Pull it from the personal hook manual (`hooks-deep-reference-2026.md`, personal tier). A weak cover kills the whole carousel.
- **Caption SEO, not hashtag piles.** Front-load the words the audience actually searches (healthcare real estate, medical office lease, dental practice purchase, the market city) in the caption and profile. Hashtags are minor now.
- **Do not clone the Facebook post (Rachel Karten).** Instagram is more visual, carousel-led, more human and behind-the-scenes, and caption-SEO'd. Same substance, a genuinely different post, never a copy-paste with the city swapped. write-content's hook-divergence check already guards the opening line; here the whole register leans a touch more human.
- **Pillars keep the niche legible (Vanessa Lau).** The last 9 to 12 posts define the account. Keep the feed tightly healthcare-CRE-local so Instagram files it correctly and the grid reads as one coherent authority at a glance.

**How this maps to the card system and the format plan:** the shared card archetypes become carousel slides; the Editorial-statement or Stat-hero card makes the strongest cover; the warm CARR register plus the personal hook manual supply the words. Video, once it clears the quality hold, runs Brock Johnson's hook-first Reels and a repeatable series; until then Instagram reach leans on carousels (the swipe re-show) and sends.

## Engagement mastery

Build every post for the send and the save first, since those are the signals that reach new people and mark reference value. End on a reason to forward or bookmark, not a generic call to action. Reply to comments and DMs quickly while a post is young, because early engagement velocity shapes how far Explore and Reels push it. Use Story stickers (polls, questions, add-yours) for lightweight interaction that keeps the existing audience warm. Comment genuinely on practice-owner and local accounts to build interaction history, which the algorithm reads even for non-followers.

---

## Cadence

- **Consistency is worth about five times the engagement** of sporadic posting, so a steady rhythm matters more than volume.
- **3 to 5 quality Reels a week** beats daily low-effort posting; the algorithm rewards watch time, not frequency.
- **Space posts out.** Posting several back to back makes Instagram hide some to balance feeds. Spread them hours apart or across days.
- **Weekday midday and mornings** tend to run strong (Wednesday midday and evening, Thursday morning are common), but test the account's own insights.
- A workable weekly shape: two or three Reels, one or two reference carousels, and near-daily Stories.

## Profile optimization

- Full CARR branding, a keyword-rich bio (what CARR does, who it helps, the market), and a searchable name field.
- A clean, on-brand grid, since new visitors judge the whole profile at a glance before following.
- Real photography and original content throughout; no reshared or watermarked material.
- Highlights that organize the reference content (lease vs buy, market, the model) so a new follower can binge the value.

---

## Guardrails (unchanged, apply on every post)

- CARR branding stays; Instagram is not a personal brand.
- The publication firewall runs on every post (no internal-only material, vendor safety, counterpart safety).
- Never impugn market counterparts; contrast the model, never the people, per brand-voice.md. The conflict-of-interest angle is allowed on Instagram, as a structural model contrast only.
- HIPAA: no patient data, no patient-identifying photography, no demographic or heat-mapping content.
- Run DNA/writing-rules.md (the zero-tolerance AI-tell list plus the humanity passes) on every caption and every on-screen or on-slide line.
- Cite any number in plain text. No external links in captions (Instagram does not make them clickable anyway); drive to the profile link or a DM.

## Measurement

Instagram feeds the same performance loop, but the scoreboard is sends, saves, and reach to non-followers, then profile visits and follows. Likes are the weakest signal. Track which formats (Reel vs carousel) and which topics actually get forwarded and saved, and let the monthly review steer the mix. Log Instagram posts to the performance log like any other.
