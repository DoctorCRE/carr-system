## The one thing to get straight first: this is not DTC

Almost every famous Facebook-ads operator built their name in direct-to-consumer e-commerce, where a stranger buys a $40 product off a cold ad in one sitting. CARR is the opposite: a niche, high-value, high-trust, infrequent B2B decision. **Nobody hires a tenant rep off a single cold ad.** So the DTC playbooks do not port directly. Their CRAFT ports (creative discipline, testing, funnel structure, the algorithm); their GOAL (immediate cold-traffic sale) does not.

**The primary goal is PRESENCE, not leads (Joe, July 18, 2026): get into the territory doctors' feeds and stay there.** The point is to be in their pocket, a familiar recurring name on the phones of the specific practice owners in this market, so that when a lease event or a purchase finally comes, CARR is already the name they know and trust. This is share-of-mind inside a defined LOCAL audience. It is not global reach and it is not immediate lead capture. Everything below serves that.

What Facebook ads do for CARR, in priority order:
1. **Presence in the territory doctors' feeds (the main job).** Show up repeatedly, cheaply, in front of the practice owners in this market. Reach and frequency inside the territory audience is the primary measure: are the right doctors seeing CARR, and often enough to remember it. An always-on, low-budget, omnipresent-in-territory posture beats occasional bursts.
2. **Capture the small slice with active intent** (a lease event inside 24 months) with a value offer.
3. **Convert the warm audience** the organic content and presence already built, by putting the no-cost-representation offer in front of people who already know the brand.

Because it is a defined-audience awareness play, it is measured FIRST in territory reach and frequency (are the target doctors seeing us, and how often), then in cost-per-qualified-lead and pipeline, never in e-commerce ROAS. One closed representation deal is worth enough that even an expensive lead pencils, but the volume is low and the cycle is long. Judge it on that timescale.

**Spend gate (standing rule):** ads spend real money, so launching or funding a campaign is always Joe's explicit decision. This file is the plan; nothing goes live or scales without Joe approving the spend. Nothing auto-fires.

## The field's playbook: what the best Facebook-ads operators actually do

A research pass on the ten most influential voices in Facebook advertising, distilled and translated to niche local B2B lead-gen. Sources at the foot.

**The ten and the one thing to take from each:**
1. **Dennis Yu** (BlitzMetrics) — Dollar-a-Day. Do not launch big cold campaigns. Boost the organic posts that already earned real engagement, a dollar or a few dollars a day, to the local audience. Amplify proven content instead of gambling on new. His 3x3 (why/how/what) and the awareness-to-engagement-to-conversion sequence are the funnel in miniature. This is the RIGHT starting posture for CARR's stage and budget.
2. **Jon Loomer** — advanced, data-driven discipline. Understand the delivery system, test deliberately, read the data, and do not fight the algorithm with manual micromanagement. Measurement is the craft.
3. **Depesh Mandalia** (SM Commerce, the BPM Method) — a systematic, persona-and-message-led approach to scaling. Know the persona cold, match the message to them, and scale in a structured way rather than by impulse.
4. **Nick Shackelford** (Structured) — creative-led media buying at volume. The account is a creative-testing machine; you win by feeding it many creatives, not by fiddling with settings. Over $100M spent taught him the creative is the lever.
5. **Savannah Sanchez** (The Social Savannah) — the creative IS the targeting. In 2026 a strong, self-selecting creative qualifies the audience better than detailed-targeting boxes do. UGC and Reels-native formats win. Make the creative call out exactly who it is for.
6. **Amanda Bond** (The Ad Strategist) — ad SEQUENCING. Do not show everyone the same ad. Connect first (awareness, story), then commit (a value offer), then close (the ask), matched to where the person is in the journey. Retargeting the same message at everyone wastes money.
7. **Molly Pittman** (DigitalMarketer) — traffic TEMPERATURE. Cold, warm, and hot audiences need different messages: cold gets education and awareness, warm gets a value offer, hot gets the direct ask. Match message to temperature.
8. **Ralph Burns** (Tier 11, Perpetual Traffic) — scaling systems and account structure. Paid traffic is an operating cadence, not a one-off. Build the structure, then scale what works methodically.
9. **Ezra Firestone** (Smart Marketer) — customer-centric, brand plus direct-response. Ads should feel like content the viewer is glad to see, blend brand warmth with a real offer, and optimize for profit and lifetime value, not vanity ROAS.
10. **Charley Tichenor** (Disruptor School) — broad targeting and campaign consolidation. Trust the algorithm with fewer, simpler, well-funded campaigns and diverse creative, rather than slicing into many tiny hyper-targeted ad sets. Simplicity plus creative volume beats micro-management.

**The consensus across all ten (2026):**
- **Creative has overtaken targeting as the number-one lever.** "The creative is the new targeting." Creative velocity is the operational advantage now: the best advertisers test many creatives, not many audiences.
- **Advantage+ and AI are the default structure.** Meta's Advantage+ campaigns report a 22 to 32% ROAS lift over manual-only, because the AI accesses broader data and optimizes faster than hand-tuning. Use it, do not fight it.
- **Clean first-party data wins.** Feed the pixel and the Conversions API clean events, and feed custom audiences a clean CRM list. Better data in, better delivery out.
- **Lead Ads (native forms) lift conversion** for local and service businesses, and high-value services can pay more per lead because each closed deal is worth so much.

## Engineering the campaigns and the funnel (the studied framework, applied)

Two people own this part of the craft, and one is almost tailor-made for CARR.

**Ryan Deiss (DigitalMarketer) built the funnel-design spine: the Customer Value Journey.** It is an 8-stage model of how a stranger becomes an advocate: Aware, Engage, Subscribe, Convert, Excite, Ascend, Advocate, Promote. The discipline it teaches is to know exactly what stage a person is at and engineer the ONE next step, never skipping ahead. You do not ask a stranger to buy; you move them one dot at a time.

**Dennis Yu (BlitzMetrics) built the Facebook-specific campaign architecture, and he built it for local service businesses on a tiny budget to make the phone ring.** That is CARR's exact situation, which is why his system is the one to engineer around. Two ideas carry it: the 3-campaign funnel and Dollar-a-Day.

### The spine: the Customer Value Journey, mapped to CARR
The ad system does not carry the whole journey. It engineers the first four stages; CARR's people and relationships carry the rest.
- **Aware** — the territory doctor first sees CARR in their feed (the presence goal).
- **Engage** — they do something: watch, click, save, read.
- **Subscribe** — they give a way to reach them: a lead-form submit, a guide download. Now CARR owns the follow-up, off the rented platform.
- **Convert** — they take the consult, the free evaluation. First real commitment.
- **Excite, Ascend, Advocate, Promote** — the deal experience, the second location, the referral to a colleague, the vendor who sends the next client. This is CARR's referral engine and network, not the ad account. The ads hand a warm, converted lead to the relationship system, which runs the rest.

### The engine: Dennis Yu's 3-campaign system, engineered for CARR
Three campaigns, one per funnel stage, each with its own job, audience, and creative. Facebook's names are awareness, consideration, conversion; Dennis Yu's shorthand is WHY, HOW, WHAT; the plain version is know, like, trust (you have to know someone before you like them, and like them before you trust them).

1. **Awareness campaign (WHY / know).** Runs to the cold territory audience. Job: presence and frequency, get in the doctors' pockets. Creative: the best organic educational content (card posts, lease lessons, local news), boosted. This is the always-on top that never turns off.
2. **Engagement campaign (HOW / like).** Runs to the people who engaged with the awareness layer. Job: deepen trust and pull the value offer. Creative: the "how it works" material and the value offer (free lease review, the guide) as a Lead Ad. This is where a stranger becomes a known contact.
3. **Conversion campaign (WHAT / trust).** Runs to the warm audience that took the offer. Job: the consult. Creative: the no-cost-representation message and the free evaluation. This is the ONLY campaign where the conflict-of-interest and no-cost message runs, because these people already know the brand and it lands as relief, not a cold pitch. Free evaluation, paid out of the deal, tenant's side only.

**The 3x3 and the nine winning pieces.** Dennis Yu's target is nine proven pieces of content, three per stage, found by testing. For CARR the 3x3 is why/how/what content on the core topics (the lease, buy-versus-lease, the local market), and the goal is to end up with a small stable of awareness, engagement, and conversion pieces that are known to work, then keep them running.

**Dollar-a-Day is the testing and scaling method, and it fits CARR's stage exactly.** Do not build big cold campaigns and hope. Boost proven organic posts at a dollar or a few dollars a day, see which actually earn engagement in the territory, and put more budget only behind the winners. It is cheap, low-risk, and was designed for a local business making the phone ring, not a DTC brand chasing ROAS. This is where CARR starts.

### How people move between the three campaigns
The mechanic: a person moves up only when they take a trackable ACTION, and the action drops them into the next campaign's audience automatically. Engagers of the awareness campaign flow into a custom audience the engagement campaign targets; offer-takers flow into the audience the conversion campaign targets. The Pixel and the Conversions API record the signals, custom audiences do the sorting, and you pre-decide what each stage sees. All three campaigns run at once, always-on, and individuals flow through at their own pace. Nobody is promoted by seeing an ad; they are promoted by doing something.

### The engineering mechanics
- **Advantage+ or CBO for the cold prospecting** (awareness), so Meta's AI finds the practice owners inside the geo-fence and moves budget to what works. **ABO for the retargeting** (conversion), where the audiences are small and high-value and you want manual control of the spend.
- **Pixel plus Conversions API on everything.** This is what stitches the three campaigns together and feeds clean signals back. Without it the funnel cannot see who to move up and the compounding breaks.
- **Budget by CARR's stage, not the DTC textbook.** The standard split (roughly half the budget on bottom-of-funnel conversion) assumes a big warm pool. CARR's warm pool starts near empty, so early budget is mostly the always-on awareness presence layer plus a small standing retargeting catch, and it shifts down-funnel only as the warm audience actually grows. Retargeting earns the first dollar per unit of value because it is the cheapest and highest-return, but there is little of it to fund early, so awareness carries the weight until the pool fills.
- **Feed proven content up the funnel.** The organic feed and the awareness campaign are the same content: the winners of the organic performance loop become the boosted awareness pieces. Paid and organic are one loop, not two efforts.

## The content that fills the system (the nine pieces)

Dennis Yu's target is nine winning pieces, three per stage. Here is what those are for CARR, and honestly, what already exists versus what has to be built. Most of the raw material is already here (the card system, the lease article, the substance bank). The one real gap is the value offer.

**Awareness (WHY / cold / get in their pocket).** Pure value and familiarity, no ask. The job is presence.
1. **Lease-lesson cards.** The stat, split-compare, and editorial cards on the numbers that matter (the escalator math, the build-out gap, the five numbers that cost more than the rent). Source: the shared card system and the substance bank. Already have it.
2. **Local market dispatch cards.** The territory news that says "your market is moving and we watch it" (the Cantonment clinic, the Baptist demolition, the jobs wave). Source: the substance bank. Already have it.
3. **The advocate piece.** A warm, light version of who CARR is and why a practice owner deserves someone on their side of the table. Source: brand voice. Have the material.

**Engagement (HOW / warm / pull the offer).** Deeper teaching plus the value offer. The job is to turn a viewer into a known contact.
4. **How a medical lease really works.** The article, repurposed as a carousel. Already built.
5. **The value offer / lead magnet.** A genuinely useful downloadable a practice owner would trade their email for: a practice-owner's lease checklist, or the "five numbers" guide as a PDF, delivered by a Lead Ad. THIS IS THE MISSING PIECE, and it is the linchpin of the whole engagement stage. Without it there is nothing to pull people off the awareness layer. Build this first.
6. **What a tenant rep actually does.** The plain-language "here is how representation works and what it changes" piece, including that it costs the tenant nothing. Have the material, needs assembling into a piece.

**Conversion (WHAT / hot / book the consult).** The offer, the proof, and the no-cost message. The job is the consult.
7. **The no-cost explainer.** How representation is paid out of the deal and why the tenant otherwise has no advocate at the table. The conflict-of-interest message, which runs ONLY here. Have the positioning.
8. **The free-evaluation offer.** The direct consult ad with a clear CTA and a simple way to book. Needs the offer asset and a booking path built.
9. **Proof and credibility.** Dell's fifteen-plus years, the vendor network, CARR's national data, and anonymized outcome types. Have the material, assemble into a proof piece; add real testimonials as they come.

**Have versus build.**
- **Have:** the card system, the lease article, the substance bank, the organic feed, the positioning and proof material. Most of the awareness and conversion content is a matter of assembling what exists into ad-shaped pieces.
- **Build, in priority order:** (1) the value offer / lead magnet plus a Lead-Ad form or simple landing page to deliver it, because the engagement stage is empty without it; (2) the free-evaluation consult offer and a booking path; (3) video versions of the winners, once the quality hold lifts.

**The rule underneath all nine:** every piece uses the shared card system for the visual and Joe's personal hooks and voice for the words, so the creative is on-brand and consistent while the copy stays distinctly Joe's and diverges from Dell's. Test them Dollar-a-Day, keep the winners, and the nine become a stable, always-on set.

## Targeting a tiny, specific, local audience

The audience is narrow: independent healthcare practice owners (dentists, physicians, vets, optometrists, chiropractors, PTs, med-spa and wellness owners) in the Florida Panhandle and South Alabama. That is a targeting challenge, and the 2026 answer is to let the CREATIVE qualify rather than over-rely on detailed-targeting boxes.

- **Geography is the non-negotiable spine.** Every campaign is fenced to the real service area (Mobile and Dothan through the Panhandle to Tallahassee) and nowhere else. The goal is presence in THIS territory's doctors' feeds, so a dollar spent reaching a practice owner outside the market is a dollar wasted. Tight geo-fencing is the whole point, which is exactly why the Special-Ad-Category flag below matters so much: if Meta misclassifies the account as Housing, tight geo-radius targeting gets disabled and the territory-only spine breaks. Verify classification before spending.
- **Let the creative self-select (Savannah Sanchez).** Open the ad with a callout that filters: "If you own a dental practice and your lease is up in the next two years..." The right person leans in; everyone else scrolls, which is fine and cheap.
- **Custom audiences are how you get "in their pocket."** The most precise way to land in specific territory doctors' feeds is a custom audience built from CARR's own data: past clients, prospects Joe has actually talked to, event and referral contacts, vendor partners. Upload that list, advertise straight to it, and build lookalikes off it and off the highest-intent engagers. IMPORTANT caveat: Meta's Custom Audience terms require a lawful basis and that the list be first-party data you have the right to use (your own contacts and customers). A cold, compiled list of territory doctors who never opted in can violate those terms, so do NOT upload scraped or purchased lists as a custom audience. For cold territory doctors Joe has no relationship with yet, reach them through geography plus profession and behavior targeting and the self-selecting creative instead, and move them into a warm custom audience only once they engage.
- **Advantage+ with guardrails.** Run Advantage+ broad within the geography and let the AI find the practice owners, rather than hand-stacking interest boxes that are thin for this niche. Feed it strong creative and clean audiences and trust it (Charley Tichenor).
- **Retargeting is the highest-value layer.** Website visitors, video-viewers, post-engagers, lead-form openers, and the CRM list are where the money works hardest.

**Compliance flag to verify at launch (do not assume): Meta Special Ad Categories.** Meta restricts targeting on ads it classifies as Housing (and Employment, Credit). That category is meant for RESIDENTIAL housing (home sale or rental, mortgages, homeowners insurance). Commercial real estate services marketed to business owners are generally NOT residential housing and should not fall under it. BUT Meta's automated review can misclassify anything real-estate-flavored, and if an ad or account gets flagged into the Housing category, detailed targeting and tight geo-radius get disabled. So at launch, confirm the classification in Ads Manager; if flagged, adjust the creative language away from anything that reads as property listing and lean harder on geography plus self-selecting creative. Flag it, verify it, do not build on an assumption.

## Creative doctrine (where the card system and the hooks do the work)

Creative is the lever, so this is where most of the effort goes.

- **The shared card system powers static and carousel ad creative.** The archetypes (stat hero, split compare, editorial statement, local dispatch, quote) are already ad-ready at 1080x1350. Static cards and carousels are the default paid creative while video is on the quality hold.
- **The hook is the ad.** The first line and the first frame decide whether the ad is seen. Pull hooks from the personal hook manual (`hooks-deep-reference-2026.md`, personal tier), tuned to the funnel stage: cold gets curiosity and problem-agitation, warm gets the value promise, hot gets the direct no-cost offer. This is where each agent's creative diverges from the other's.
- **Creative velocity, scaled to CARR.** The DTC "20 to 50 creatives a week" is not the CARR reality, but the principle holds: test several creatives, keep the winners, retire the rest, and treat the ad account as a testing machine, not a set-and-forget. Two to three variants per test so each gets enough spend to signal.
- **UGC-style video is the format to unlock.** The winning 2026 ad format is authentic, UGC-style video, which for CARR means Joe on camera talking to practice owners in plain language. That is gated behind the standing video quality hold (no embarrassingly-AI output); when real footage clears the bar, video becomes the primary ad creative. Until then, static and carousel carry it.
- **Ads should feel like the content, not like an ad (Ezra Firestone).** The same warm, educational register as the organic feed. A "the Pensacola market is hot, call now" ad reads as spam; a "here are the five numbers that cost more than your rent" ad earns the click.

## Budget and measurement

- **Start small and bottom-up.** Dollar-a-Day boosting of proven posts plus a small retargeting budget is the right first spend. Scale only what converts.
- **Primary metric is presence in the territory: reach and frequency.** Because the main goal is being in the doctors' pockets, the first scoreboard is how many of the territory's practice owners the ads reached and how often they saw CARR (frequency). Watch that the frequency stays high enough to build familiarity but not so high it fatigues, and that reach stays inside the territory. This is the awareness half of the account and it is judged on presence, not leads.
- **Then measure cost-per-qualified-lead and pipeline, not ROAS.** E-commerce ROAS is the wrong scoreboard for a considered service with a long cycle. Track cost per lead, lead-to-consult rate, consult-to-engagement rate, and eventually cost per signed representation. A high cost-per-lead can still be a bargain when a closed deal is worth what a CRE deal is worth.
- **Give it time and volume before judging.** Low lead volume means small samples; do not kill a campaign on a week of thin data. Let each creative and audience accumulate real signal first.
- **Feed the loop.** Ad performance joins the same performance loop as organic; log spend, leads, and outcomes so the monthly review steers budget on evidence.

## Compliance and guardrails (apply to every ad)

- **Spend is always Joe's call.** Nothing launches or scales without explicit approval. Nothing auto-fires.
- **HIPAA is absolute.** No patient data, no patient-identifying imagery, no health-condition targeting, no heat-map or demographic-study content in an ad. Ever.
- **Special Ad Category check at launch** (see Targeting). Verify classification; do not assume.
- **CARR branding stays.** Paid is not a personal brand; the CARR-branded page runs the ads. (X, Joe's personal brand, does not run these.)
- **Conflict-of-interest message is retargeting-only.** It lands with warm audiences who know the brand; it does not go in cold ads, and it stays a structural model contrast, never an attack on a counterpart.
- **Writing rules run on every ad,** including text inside the creative: no em-dashes, no AI-tell vocabulary, no banned constructions. Cite any number plainly.
- **Publication and vendor firewall** applies: no internal tactics, no naming a competitor or a specific DSO or PE firm.

## Andromeda-era update (merged 2026-07-31 from Cody Schneider's live class, youtu.be/DbUzpz-_Yew + its outline post; watched via the transcribe-distill-discard pipeline, full read on the gold rule. Presenter's case study is B2B lead-gen, not ecom, which is why this merges rather than parks.)

- **Interest targeting is dead; the creative IS the targeting.** Meta's Andromeda matches on the ad + landing page content, so audience selection is now done by LANGUAGE: many distinct persona/angle creatives, each written in the exact words its doctor-segment uses. Mine that language where practice owners actually complain (dental/vet/chiro groups, niche subreddits, forums) BEFORE writing copy — the manual version (no API stack) works day one.
- **Structure:** a testing campaign (5 ads per ad set fighting for budget; Meta surfaces the winner itself) → winners graduate to their own campaign, watched for fatigue, optimized on a DEEPER conversion event than testing → fatigued winners remix back into testing. Start events shallow (a signup Meta can see in volume), deepen only as volume allows, never more than ~5 conversion events total.
- **Budgets, his own local-business numbers:** ~$30/day is the local floor, and two weeks is the SIGNAL window, not the result window (his $100→$53 CPL case took four). Plan tests as month-shaped.
- **THE most transferable idea — long-cycle lead scoring by proxy:** waiting for closed-won to train Meta FAILS on long cycles (his words, for a mere 4-week cycle; ours is longer). Instead: profile the best PAST clients on secondary signals (practice web traffic, review count/velocity, firm shape), build the "shape of a good client," score each new lead against it in real time, and only 6+/10 fires back to Meta as the qualified event. Conceptually ready for CARR whenever ads start; the enrichment can even be manual at our volume.
- **Stage-honest scope ruling (presenter's own answer at small scale): NO always-on agent, no warehouse, no ClickHouse.** At CARR's volume the right build is the existing local session driving creative + uploads (his "coding agent as copilot" answer is literally our architecture); the 4-6-week warehouse build is for national/ecom volume. If ever built: Marketing API for WRITES ONLY (analytics pulls risk ToS bans), analysis from a warehouse, system-user token.
- **Creative entropy is real and admitted unsolved** — agents left alone regenerate the same ad; fresh DNA comes from competitor ad libraries + trending-format scrapes. Cheap models test, expensive models scale only proven formats. (AI-avatar UGC stack noted — HeyGen + ElevenLabs — but our demoted-faceless ruling stands for organic; a paid-unit exception would be its own Joe decision.)
