## What Facebook is for (the goal)

Facebook is the relationship and local-trust platform. It is not where a healthcare CRE deal closes and it is not a broadcast billboard. It is where a CARR-branded local presence builds familiarity with the practice owners, staff, and community in a specific market, so that when someone needs tenant or buyer representation, the name is already known and trusted. The audience is local and human: doctors, dentists, vets, optometrists, their teams, and the surrounding community.

Success on Facebook is measured in meaningful local engagement and reach, not follower count. The actions that matter are comments, shares, and saves from real people in the market, because those are what the 2026 algorithm reads as genuine interest and what actually signals a warm audience. A post that starts a real back-and-forth with three local practice owners is worth more than one that collects fifty passive likes from strangers.

---

## What the 2026 Facebook algorithm rewards

Four signals drive reach now, and they all point the same direction: original, niche, genuinely engaging content from a Page that posts consistently.

**Originality, enforced by fingerprinting.** Meta now preferentially promotes content the Page filmed or produced itself, and it uses pattern-based video fingerprinting to track which Page uploaded a piece first. Low-effort reposts, stitches, and reaction clips with no real commentary are demoted. Reshared stock and other people's content is the weakest thing you can post. Original photos and video of your actual market are the strongest.

**Relevance, scored by a survey.** Facebook launched the User True Interest Survey, which asks viewers to rate on a 1 to 5 scale how well a Reel matched their interests. This lets Facebook tell genuine interest from passive scrolling, and it means niche, audience-specific content now outscores broad, generic content. A post aimed squarely at "independent dental practice owners in this metro" beats a vague "healthcare providers" post, because the people it reaches rate it as a strong match.

**Meaningful interactions over volume.** The algorithm rewards comments, shares, and saves far more than likes, because those are deeper signals. It favors content that feels genuine and relatable over branded, product-centric promotion. Posting more does not help; posting things people actually respond to does.

**Reels freshness.** Reels are the growth surface. Their views and watch time roughly doubled in late 2025, and Meta surfaces around 50% more Reels from creators who published that same day, so freshness carries a real bonus. The first three seconds decide whether a Reel is watched.

## Format doctrine (what to post, in priority order)

1. **Original Reels** are the reach engine. Real footage of the actual market (property, streets, the kind of small practice spaces CARR clients occupy), a hook in the first three seconds, on-screen text since most watch muted, kept short. Follow the video lane rules already established (real footage default, every scene earns its place, no AI tells, CARR brand overlay). One Reel a week is the starting cadence, tuned up or down by the monthly performance review.
2. **Original local photos and simple graphics** for market activity and proof: a real building, a submarket, a clean data card. Original beats stock every time under fingerprinting.
3. **Interactive posts** (a genuine question, a poll) to pull comments, which are the heaviest engagement signal.
4. **Carousels** for multi-point education a reader can swipe through.
5. **Text-only posts** are the lightest format here and should be the exception, used when a short local observation or question stands on its own.

Never reshare other people's content as filler. A reaction or remix only counts as original if it adds real analysis or a new angle.

## What to post (CARR pillars, Facebook-native)

The three content pillars and the 40/30/30 mix still govern the topic split; Facebook changes the delivery and the register, not the substance. Facebook is the one platform where direct-to-doctor tenant advice and the conflict-of-interest angle run well (unlike LinkedIn, where they stay off).

- **Local Market Activity (30%):** new practice openings and expansions in the market, submarket commentary, real photos of the territory, local healthcare real estate news. This is Facebook's strongest lane because it is original, local, and relevant all at once.
- **Educational / Market Authority (40%):** plain tenant and buyer advice a practice owner can use (lease versus buy, what a TI allowance really means, occupancy cost, due-diligence pitfalls), and the no-conflict model explained as a model contrast, never as an attack on counterparts.
- **Brand / Behind-the-Scenes (30%):** the CARR mission ("helping people who help people"), the tenant-and-buyer-only model, the local team, and the vendor network, in a warm and human register.

Niche every post to a specific vertical or submarket where possible, because the relevance survey rewards it. "What a dental practice owner in this area should know before signing" outperforms "healthcare providers should know their lease."

## The field's playbook: what the best Facebook operators actually do (added July 18, 2026)

A research pass on the ten most influential voices in Facebook and Page content, distilled to the techniques that transfer to a local healthcare-CRE Page. The algorithm section above is WHAT the platform rewards; this is HOW the best practitioners earn it. Sources at the foot of the file.

**The ten and the one thing to take from each:**
1. **Mari Smith** (the "Queen of Facebook") — the plain-text-on-color QUESTION post. Meta Business Suite's text-on-background format reads to the algorithm as a status update but stops the scroll like a graphic. Run one a few times a week with a real question aimed at a specific local audience. Also hers: judge posts by FEEDBACK PERCENTAGE (reactions plus comments over impressions), not raw likes.
2. **Dennis Yu** (BlitzMetrics) — the 3x3 topic engine and "amplify what already works." Every topic splits into WHY, HOW, and WHAT, which is three posts from one idea. Do not guess at new content; find the post that already earned real engagement and make more like it. (His Dollar-a-Day is a paid-amplification play; the organic lesson is to let the winners tell you what to make next.)
3. **Gary Vaynerchuk** — jab, jab, jab, right hook. Give value many times before you ask once, roughly nine to one. The ratio is the strategy, not the pitch. Most posts here are pure value; the occasional ask (a consult, "message me") is earned by the giving. Also: document, do not create. Real market observation beats produced-feeling content.
4. **Donald Miller** (StoryBrand) — the customer is the HERO, you are the GUIDE. Never cast CARR as the hero of the story. The doctor is the hero with a problem (a lease they are about to sign alone); CARR is the guide with a plan. Clarity over cleverness: if they have to work to understand it, they are gone.
5. **Katie Lance** (#GetSocialSmart, real-estate-specific) — content PILLARS on a grid, and own your ground. Social is "rented ground"; the durable asset is original commentary (your photos, your market reads) that belongs to you. Plan in pillars, not one-off posts. Get on camera and tell the backstory.
6. **Jon Loomer** — data-driven discipline. Read the Page's own insights, test format and timing, and steer from what THIS Page's audience actually does, not from a generic best-practice list. Ties straight into the performance loop.
7. **Ted Rubin** — Return on Relationship (#RonR). The reply is the content. Reach is a proxy; the relationship is the asset. Reply to every comment, comment first on local Pages, treat the Page as a two-way channel.
8. **Amy Porterfield** — teach on a schedule and batch it. Lead with genuine education that stands alone (useful even if they never hire you), and batch-produce so the cadence never breaks.
9. **Mike Stelzner** (Social Media Examiner) — serve, do not sell; elevate others. Feature the market, the partners, and the community more than yourself. Consistency compounds.
10. **Jeff Bullas** — headline and first-line craft. The opening line does most of the work; write it like a headline, not a warm-up.

**What this changes about how a post is BUILT here (the upgrade):**
- **Hooks: same anatomy, each agent's own bank.** Every hook is a callout plus curiosity-or-promise plus a concrete detail, tuned WARM for Facebook's local audience (problem-aware and solution-aware hooks work here, not only cold curiosity) in the CARR "we" register. The universal anatomy and the two per-platform dials are in the shared craft bank (`marketing-playbook.md` Hooks section). The dialed-in pattern bank and swipe file are each agent's OWN, kept personal by design so Joe's and Dell's Pages do not converge (Joe, July 18, 2026). Build and keep your own.
- **The first line is a headline, and it has to land in ~125 characters.** That is where Facebook cuts to "See more" on mobile. Say the thing, or open the curiosity gap, before the fold. Never bury the point under a warm-up sentence.
- **Pick a proven skeleton, do not freestyle.** Three that fit this Page: PAS (Problem, Agitate, Solution) names the lease mistake, sits in what it costs, then gives the fix; BAB (Before, After, Bridge) shows the doctor negotiating alone, then with an advocate, then how you get from one to the other; StoryBrand-guide runs hero, problem, guide, plan, outcome.
- **Standing format, the text question post (Mari Smith):** a few times a week, a plain-text-on-color post in Meta Business Suite asking one real question of a specific local audience. Cheap to make, heavy on comments, and comments are the top reach signal. It is also a variety break from the graphic cards.
- **The 3x3 topic engine (Dennis Yu):** when a topic lands, split it into why/how/what and get three spaced-out posts from it. Fills the calendar without inventing filler.
- **You are the guide, never the hero (StoryBrand):** the doctor is the hero of every post. Rewrite any line that makes CARR the protagonist.
- **The reply is the job (Ted Rubin, Loomer):** first-hour replies, comment on local Pages, and measure feedback percentage over raw likes.

**How this maps to the card system (the July 18 lookbook, `Marketing/Social Media/card-system-lookbook-2026-07-18.png`):** frameworks and visuals pair up, which is also how the feed stays varied.
- Stat hero card → a number-led PAS or BAB post.
- Split compare card → a Before/After contrast.
- Editorial statement card → a StoryBrand stakes line or a value jab.
- Photo band / Local dispatch cards → local market activity (document, do not create).
- Quote/principle card → a standalone teaching line.
- Text question post → no card at all; the Mari Smith format is its own variety break.

## Engagement mastery

Write every post to earn a comment, a share, or a save from a real local person, since those are the signals that move reach. End on a genuine question aimed at a specific local audience, not a manufactured call to action. Reply to every comment quickly while the post is young, because the first hour of engagement shapes the post's reach and because two-way conversation is exactly what Facebook's relevance system is built to reward. Comment genuinely on local practices', partners', and community pages' posts; reciprocity is how a local Facebook presence compounds.

Avoid engagement bait ("comment YES"), which is suppressed, and avoid product-centric promotional posts, which the algorithm now actively demotes. The goal is a real conversation with a specific market, not a broadcast.

---

## Cadence

- **3 to 5 posts a week**, quality over volume. More generic posts hurt rather than help.
- **Weekday mornings and midday** are the common windows (Wednesday through Friday tend to run strong), but there is no universal best time. Post when the local audience is active and let the Page's own insights settle it.
- **Space posts out** across the week rather than clustering them.
- **Consistency beats bursts.** A steady, predictable rhythm compounds; a big week followed by silence does not.
- **About one Reel a week** to start, then let the monthly review set the video-to-static ratio from what actually engages.

---

## Groups and local community

Facebook Groups and local community pages are a quiet strength for a relationship-driven local business, and they are underused. Engage genuinely in local practice-owner, small-business, and community groups where it is appropriate, and consider building a CARR-adjacent local community over time. Two-way conversation in a group turns members into familiar contacts and advocates. This is relationship groundwork, not a place to drop links or pitches.

## Profile optimization

The Page is the storefront, so it has to be complete and credible:
- Full CARR branding (logo, cover, navy and orange), consistent with the other CARR surfaces.
- Accurate business hours, service area, website, and contact information, since local Pages with complete, verified details win trust and rank better in a competitive local market.
- A clear one-line description of what CARR does and the market served.
- Real photography over stock throughout.

---

## Links, video, and mechanics

- **No external links in the post body.** Links suppress reach. If a link is genuinely needed, put it in a comment or share it on request. Keep the substance native to the post.
- **Facebook is a video-welcome surface.** Reels are the primary growth format here, alongside static. Real footage is the default per the video lane runbook; every clip must be topically relevant and free of AI tells.
- **Originality is a hard rule.** Original photos and short video of the actual market beat stock or reshares under fingerprinting.

---

## Guardrails (unchanged, apply on every post)

- CARR branding stays; Facebook is not a personal brand.
- The publication firewall runs on every post (no internal-only material, vendor safety, competitor and counterpart safety).
- Never impugn market counterparts. Contrast the model, never the people, per the pulled-post rules in brand-voice.md. The conflict-of-interest angle is allowed on Facebook, but only as a structural model contrast.
- HIPAA: no patient data, no patient-identifying photography, no demographic or heat-mapping content.
- Run DNA/writing-rules.md (the zero-tolerance AI-tell list plus the humanity passes) on every post, including any text inside a graphic or on a Reel.
- Cite any number in plain text (CBRE, U.S. Census). A light source name is enough.

---

## Measurement

Facebook feeds the same performance loop as everything else, but the scoreboard is local engagement, not vanity totals. Track comments, shares, and saves first (the deep signals), then local reach and Page profile actions (visits, calls, direction taps). Let the monthly review steer topic, format, and the Reel ratio from what actually engaged the local market. Log Facebook posts to the performance log like any other.
