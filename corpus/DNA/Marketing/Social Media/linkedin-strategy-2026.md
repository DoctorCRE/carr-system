## What LinkedIn is for (the goal)

LinkedIn is the referral-hub and professional-authority platform. The audience here is not doctors; it is local agents, lenders, equipment and supply reps, attorneys, CPAs, contractors, architects, and other referral partners. Doctors are not active on LinkedIn, so this is not a direct-to-client channel. It is where CARR becomes the recognized market expert and the center of a referral network, so that partners think of CARR first and send clients its way.

Because the audience is vendors and peers, the register is professional, educational, and safe. This is the one platform where the sharp opinion and the conflict-of-interest angle stay off, because they would antagonize the local agents and counterparts CARR closes deals with every week. Success on LinkedIn is measured in meaningful professional engagement (substantive comments, saves, and reshares from partners) and in the conversations and introductions that follow, not in follower count or viral reach.

## Post from the personal profile, not the company page

LinkedIn heavily favors personal profiles over company pages, on the order of a personal profile reaching a large share of its network while a company page reaches only a few percent. So the CARR presence on LinkedIn runs through the individual's personal profile (Joe's, Dell's), with the CARR affiliation as professional credibility, not through a branded company page. Make sure the profile headline, About section, and experience all reinforce the same expert positioning the content builds.

## What the 2026 LinkedIn algorithm rewards

LinkedIn scores a post through a sequence: a spam and quality filter, then engagement signals, then network relationships, then interest matching.

**Deep engagement over surface engagement.** A save can drive roughly five times the reach of a like, and about twice that of a comment. Substantive comments and reshares are the heavy signals. Likes barely move anything.

**Dwell time.** How long someone reads before scrolling is a major passive signal, which is why formats that hold a reader (document carousels, longer valuable text) outperform. Write to be read slowly.

**Relevance over recency, with a long tail.** Unlike the other platforms, LinkedIn keeps evaluating a post for weeks, and a strong post from two or three weeks ago can resurface when it matches someone's professional interests. Early engagement helps, but it does not decide everything, so evergreen professional value keeps paying out.

**Consistency and a tight topic focus.** Posting weekly gives about a 2x lift in engagement, and sticking to two or three core topics builds recognized authority. LinkedIn deliberately does not chase virality; it rewards content that drives genuine professional value and dialogue.

**Native, on-platform content.** External links reduce reach; LinkedIn deprioritizes posts that send people off-platform. Keep the substance native and put any link in a comment.

## Format doctrine (what to post, ranked for this goal)

1. **Document (PDF) carousels are the hero format.** They pull the most reach and the highest professional conversion, and they maximize dwell time because people swipe through slowly. This matches the existing doctrine: 6 to 9 slides (keep it under about 10, where engagement drops), one idea per slide, slide one hooks, the middle slides deliver the value, the last slide has a soft prompt. Use these for multi-point education aimed at partners: the load-factor breakdown, what a TI allowance really means, the five vendors around a deal, the DIY Deal composite.
2. **Text posts** carry the widest raw reach and are the workhorse. 150 to 300 words, a strong first line before the "see more" fold, line breaks for readability, opened by an educational or data-backed hook and closed with a real discussion question. This is where vendor pain-point content lives.
3. **Native video**, 45 to 90 seconds, builds authority and converts well, and uploads are growing fast. Use it when an angle genuinely suits the partner audience, which the existing doctrine notes is relatively rare here; captioned, brand shown early, hook in the first three seconds.
4. **Polls** generate high surface engagement but shallow value (most of it is just voting), so use them sparingly, no more than every couple of weeks, and only when the question genuinely helps.
5. **Newsletters and longer articles** for evergreen authority, given LinkedIn's long content tail, when there is a topic worth the depth.

## What to post (the vendor and referral-hub content)

The content pillars still apply, but LinkedIn's audience reshapes them toward the referral network. The defining LinkedIn genre is content written to a vendor category about their world, not to the doctor.

- **Vendor pain-point content by type.** Speak to each partner type's real pain points: bankers (approved loans that never close, client retention), supply and equipment reps (turnover, quota pressure, clients with no cash left for equipment after a bad lease), attorneys and CPAs (clients who sign first and call second). Source the psychology from the Network type-playbooks (DNA/Network/vendor-relationship-craft.md). This is what makes the profile a referral hub rather than another agent feed. Firewall: use the pain-point insight only; never expose how CARR actually works those relationships.
- **Vendor-category value-add posts** are a standing genre, roughly one in four or five posts, cycling through lender, equipment supplier, CPA, attorney, contractor, and architect so no single type feels singled out. Each shows why CARR protecting the tenant's economics makes that partner's outcome better, and closes as a referral-loop invitation.
- **Market commentary and industry data** that position the poster as the market expert peers compare notes with.
- **Educational frameworks** partners can use and reshare.

Keep the conflict-of-interest angle and any competitor criticism off LinkedIn entirely. Contrast the model only where unavoidable, never the people, and never on this platform if it risks a local-agent connection.

## The field's playbook: what the best LinkedIn operators actually do (added July 18, 2026)

A research pass on the ten most influential voices in LinkedIn content, distilled to what transfers to a founder-led healthcare-CRE presence whose audience is referral partners and vendors, not practice owners. The algorithm section above is WHAT the platform rewards; this is HOW the best operators earn it. Sources at the foot.

**The ten and the one thing to take from each:**
1. **Richard van der Blom** (the LinkedIn Algorithm Insights Report, 1.3M+ posts analyzed) — the platform shifted from viral reach to DEPTH AND AUTHORITY. Dwell time is the signal, not the click. Document carousels are the highest-engagement format. LinkedIn distributes by your "topic DNA," meaning demonstrated expertise, so a tight and consistent subject is what makes it file you as the authority. One external link in the body cuts reach about 19%, so links go in a comment.
2. **Justin Welsh** (Content OS) — the TRAILER: everything visible before the "...more" cutoff has one job, break the scroll and earn the click. Systematize it, ideate in short batches, produce consistently, and repurpose what already worked. His storytelling posts open on a pain.
3. **Lara Acosta** (LinkedIn writing) — the SLAY framework: Story, Lesson, Actionable, You. Open on a short story, distill it to one lesson, give a few concrete steps, close with a question to the reader. Her hook patterns: curiosity drops, specificity stacks, expectation breaks, counter-intuitive reframes. Write to one reader.
4. **Dave Gerhardt** (B2B, Exit Five) — share what you are LEARNING as you work: quick write-ups after client and market conversations, and content about the life and job of the person you are trying to reach. For a partner audience that means posts about a lender's, CPA's, or contractor's world, not only your own.
5. **Daniel Priestley** (Key Person of Influence) — become the go-to person in one industry through pitch, publish, product, profile, partnership. LinkedIn is the publish-and-profile engine, and the partnership pillar is the whole referral game.
6. **Sahil Bloom** — take one idea and package it simply. A single clear framework or mental model, well-distilled, out-travels a dense post. One idea per post.
7. **Josh Braun** (selling without the ick) — anti-pitch. Start a conversation, do not broadcast a sale. Curiosity and genuine usefulness over "here is what we do," and never needy. This fits the partner audience exactly, where a hard pitch reads as the wrong move.
8. **Gary Vaynerchuk** — document, do not create; give value many times before an ask; consistency over bursts.
9. **Amy Porterfield** — educate first, with material that stands alone, then nurture toward a next step.
10. **Katie Lance** (real-estate-native) — content pillars and OWNED commentary. Social is rented ground; the real asset is original market reads that belong to you. Get on camera and tell the backstory.

**What this changes about how a LinkedIn post is BUILT here (the upgrade):**
- **This is a founder-led AUTHORITY and RELATIONSHIP play, not lead-gen to doctors.** The audience is partners: lenders, CPAs, attorneys, contractors, architects, equipment reps, other professionals. The goal is to be the healthcare-CRE name they think of and refer to (Priestley's go-to person). Write to them, not to practice owners.
- **Document carousels are the power format.** They carry the highest engagement and the most dwell time on LinkedIn, and dwell time is the ranking signal. The shared CARD SYSTEM is already the exact size LinkedIn wants (portrait 1080x1350, 4:5): the archetypes become carousel slides, 7 to 12 of them, one point each, a hook cover, a takeaway close. Aim for a post a reader spends 30-plus seconds inside.
- **The Trailer is the hook (Justin Welsh).** The first two or three lines before "...more" decide the click. Same hook craft as everywhere (callout plus curiosity-or-promise plus a concrete detail), from the personal hook manual, in the professional register, kept partner-appropriate.
- **Share what you are learning (Gerhardt), aimed at the partner (Priestley's partnership pillar).** This is the vendor-category genre the file already carries, sharpened: a post about what a market shift means for a lender's borrowers, what a closed TI gap means for a contractor's scope, what early involvement gets an architect. Content about THEIR job, with CARR as the connective tissue.
- **Depth and authority, one topic (van der Blom).** Keep the subject tightly healthcare CRE so LinkedIn's topic-DNA files Joe as the authority. Educational posts with a real framework or real data get amplified to the people who care.
- **Reply discipline is not optional.** The first hour on a 2 to 5% test group decides distribution, and the top creators reply to comments far more than average, which correlates with growth. Engage partners' posts first, and stay present in the comments on your own.
- **Anti-pitch (Josh Braun) and no conflict-of-interest hot take (standing guardrail).** The tension here is professional insight, never the broker-bias callout, which stays off LinkedIn because real listing brokers Joe deals with are connections. Start conversations; do not sell at the partner.

**How this maps to the card system and the vendor genre:** the shared card archetypes become LinkedIn document-carousel slides at their native 4:5 size; the vendor-category value-add posts (lender, equipment supplier, CPA, attorney, contractor, architect) are the flagship content, roughly one in four or five posts, cycling through the categories; the personal hook manual and Joe's professional register supply the words. Video, once off the quality hold, is native LinkedIn video; until then the carousel and the single insight post carry the account.

## Engagement mastery

Treat comments as the product. End every post with a question a referral partner would actually answer, then reply to every comment early to keep the thread alive, since creator participation and dwell in the comments both lift reach. Comment genuinely and substantively on partners' and peers' posts; reciprocity is how a LinkedIn network compounds, and it is often more valuable than posting. Avoid engagement bait ("comment YES"), pods, and generic AI comments, all of which are suppressed. The outcomes LinkedIn prioritizes are professional ones, so content that leads to a real conversation, an introduction, or a partnership is exactly what the platform pushes.

---

## Cadence

- **Post consistently; weekly alone roughly doubles engagement.** A steady 2 to 4 posts a week is plenty on LinkedIn, since this is a depth platform, not a volume one.
- **Rough format rhythm:** text posts 3 to 4 times a week, a document carousel once or twice a week, video 1 to 2 times a week only when an angle fits, a document no more than weekly, polls every couple of weeks at most.
- **Weekday mornings** run strong (Tuesday and Wednesday especially), but the long content tail means timing matters less here than on any other platform. Test the profile's own analytics.
- **Two or three core topics**, held consistently, build the authority faster than range does.

## Guardrails (unchanged, apply on every post)

- Professional, CARR-affiliated register. Not a personal opinion brand; no hot takes here.
- Conflict-of-interest content is off LinkedIn, and competitor or counterpart criticism never appears, since this audience includes the agents and partners CARR works across from.
- The publication firewall runs on every post: the vendor pain-point insight is content, but how CARR actually works reps, bankers, and partners stays internal; no internal-only material; vendor safety (never name a vendor negatively, never rank named vendors, never take sides in a rivalry).
- HIPAA: no patient data or patient-identifying material.
- Run DNA/writing-rules.md (the zero-tolerance AI-tell list plus the humanity passes) on every post and every slide.
- Cite any number in plain text. No external links in the post body; put a link in a comment if truly needed.

## Measurement

LinkedIn feeds the same performance loop, but the scoreboard is depth, not reach: substantive comments, saves, and reshares from partners, plus the introductions and conversations a post generates. Saves and reshares are the signals to chase. Track which topics and formats (document carousel versus text versus vendor-category angle) actually earn partner engagement, and let the monthly review steer the mix. Log LinkedIn posts to the performance log like any other.
