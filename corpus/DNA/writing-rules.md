## Zero-Tolerance AI-Tell List (added July 6, 2026)

*Prompted by a direct conversation with Joe: whether a "tell" is fair criticism of the writing is beside the point — what matters is whether a reader primed to spot AI copy pattern-matches on it and dismisses the whole piece before the point lands. That risk exists per-instance, not per-frequency, so these are hard bans, not "use sparingly" guidance. This sits ABOVE the existing Pre-Delivery Self-Check (DNA/templates.md / write-content/SKILL.md) and the writing-audit judgment-based pass — apply this list first; it doesn't replace those, it precedes them.*

Applies to every piece of writing Claude produces or edits for Joe, on any surface — outreach email, social copy, proposals, web copy, any client- or public-facing writing. **Scope clarified July 8, 2026: this includes branded artifacts, diagram documents, lead magnets, and marketing HTML — if a prospect could ever see it, it is a writing surface.** (Added after two diagram documents shipped carrying 72 em-dashes because they were treated as design, not writing.) A single instance of any item below is a Critical finding in a writing-audit review, full stop — it does not get weighed against how well the rest of the piece reads.

**Banned outright — no exceptions, no "but it read fine here":**
- Em dash (—). Already a hard ban in outreach (DNA/templates.md); this extends the same ban to all client- and public-facing writing, including social copy.
- Flagged vocabulary: delve, unlock/unleash, harness, navigate (as a metaphor for handling a topic), elevate, unveil, revolutionize, supercharge/turbocharge, skyrocket, catapult, seamless, cutting-edge, game-changing, transformative, holistic, tapestry, landscape (as metaphor), realm, testament, myriad, plethora, treasure trove, craft/crafting (as a verb for writing or building something).
- 2026 additions (this list grows as new ones get identified): quietly (false-drama modifier), shift (vague verb standing in for a specific change), shape (vague verb), land (as in "this will land differently"), actually (filler), real as an unearned intensifier ("a real difference"), earn applied to abstractions ("earn trust"), the work (vague reverent noun), hold space, signal (abstract noun for evidence/indication), compound (as a growth metaphor), built different. July 22, 2026 additions (Claude, cross-checked against Peter Yang's open-sourced no-ai-slop pattern list; only net-new items merged): foster, facilitate, empower, streamline, robust, paradigm shift, beacon, multifaceted, meticulous, intricate, paramount, embark, ever-evolving.
- The contrast-reframe construction: "It's not about X, it's about Y" / "That's not X, that's Y" / "No X. No Y. Just Z." **Includes the compressed form (added 2026-07-14, Joe caught it in a live post): "[subject] can X, not Y" or "X, not Y" dropped straight into a sentence with no "it's"/"that's" framing** — e.g. "they can copy the practice, not the real estate." Same tell, just a shorter setup: the giveaway is the false-dichotomy shape, not the specific wording around it.
- Rule-of-three used as a rhetorical device more than once in a single piece (triads like "convenient, efficient, and innovative"). One incidental three-item list is normal prose; a piece leaning on the cadence repeatedly is the tell.
- Generic AI openers: "In today's fast-paced world," "In an era of," "In a world where," "Ever wondered," "Picture this," "Here's the deal/truth/kicker," "Let's dive in," "Welcome to the world of."
- Corporate clichés (existing ban, DNA/templates.md/write-content — restated here as part of the same list): synergy, leverage (as a verb), utilize, touch base, circle back, value-add, drive impact, unlock value, strategic alignment, operational excellence.
- Mirrored sentence skeletons (added July 8, 2026): three or more nearby sentences built on the same frame ("It can X. It can Y. It can Z." / "The A does this. The B does that."). One deliberate contrast pair is fine and often the point; stacked mirroring is the tell.
- Bullets or mid-sentence bold text on a surface meant to read as one person talking to another (outreach email, social caption) — fine in a proposal or one-pager, not here.
- **July 22, 2026 additions (Claude; merged from a diff against Peter Yang's open-sourced no-ai-slop skill — each carries a per-instance signature a primed reader can point at, so they join the hard-ban list, not the judgment list):**
  - Colon reveal: noun phrase, colon, dramatic payoff ("The best part: it learns." / "The detail that makes it work: a second pass"). Colons are for lists, labels, and quotes; write the sentence plainly.
  - Trailing -ing fake analysis: a clause bolted on with highlighting / underscoring / showcasing / reflecting / signaling to assert meaning ("...expanded the ED, underscoring its commitment to the region"). State the actual consequence or cut the clause.
  - Importance puffery: "marks a pivotal moment," "plays a vital role," "stands as a testament," "solidifies its position," "underscores the significance." State the fact; the reader decides whether it matters.
  - Weasel attribution: "experts agree," "studies show," "industry reports suggest," "many argue," "widely regarded as" with no named source. Name the source or cut the claim — same cite-or-cut rule the content fuel engine already enforces.
  - Faux-insight and rhetorical setups (extends the generic-openers ban): "what nobody tells you," "the part everyone misses," "what most people get wrong," "what if I told you," "plot twist:", "think about it:".
  - Dramatic fragmentation: stacked punch fragments as a rhythm device ("X. And Y. And Z." / "That's it. That's the whole thing.").
  - Fake-profound kicker endings: closing a piece on a manufactured aphorism or mic-drop metaphor ("The future isn't coming. It's already here."). End on the last concrete point or the next action.
  - Empty-phrase additions: "it's worth noting," "it's important to note," "at the end of the day," "the reality is," "the truth is," "at its core."

**Not on this list — judgment calls, not hard bans:** genericness, hedged/over-qualified claims, false balance, missing specifics (no real name, number, or date), uniform sentence rhythm (vary lengths; short-long-short beats machine-even cadence — added July 8, 2026), synonym cycling (rotating terms for style when repeating the clear word would be plainer — added July 22, 2026 from the Yang diff), summary-recap endings that restate the piece, and "serves as" constructions where "is" would do.

**Severely limited — not banned, but rationed (Joe, July 22, 2026):** the filler adverbs *just, literally, honestly, simply, truly, fundamentally, importantly, crucially, inherently, inevitably*. These are normal human speech, so they stay legal — but at most ONE across a whole piece, and only when it carries real emphasis, uncertainty, or the author's spoken rhythm. Two or more in one piece is a writing-audit finding. ("Actually" stays on the hard-ban list above — it was banned before this tier existed.) These are real quality problems and stay in the existing Pre-Delivery Self-Check / writing-audit review — they just don't carry a one-word signature a reader can point to and call "AI," so they're graded, not banned.

## Branding hard rules (Joe, July 10, 2026)
- **CARR is always all caps** — never Carr or carr in any written output. It's the brand.
- **A.I. is always written all caps with periods** (A.I., not AI or ai) in our own copy, titles, and system names. Exception: technical identifiers that can't carry periods or caps (file paths, slugs, the existing folder names "CARR AI" / "CARR AI Dell" — renaming those is a structural change requiring a team decision, see dna-protocol rule 13).

## Claims about Joe and Dell — hard ban (Joe, July 16, 2026)

Standing fact on every turn, including internal work: this is our vendor network, shared by Joe and Dell. Dell's 15+ years of healthcare experience is his differentiator; it does not make the network his alone.

**Default: all of Joe's marketing is solo-Joe — do not mention Dell at all.** Joe and Dell each market themselves individually most of the time; partner or "we as partners" framing is opt-in and used ONLY when Joe explicitly directs it for a specific piece. Writing as Joe, first person is "I / my" (or the firm "we"), never "Joe and Dell." The vendor and referral network is "our network" (or "my network"), NEVER "Dell's network" and NEVER credited to Dell or his years of experience. Banned constructions in any client- or public-facing content, no exceptions:
- "Dell's [N] years in healthcare / in the industry built (or helped build) the / a vendor network"
- "Dell brings [N] years ... and a vendor network"
- Any phrasing that credits the network's existence or its trustworthiness to Dell's individual tenure or years of experience.

Call the network "our network" (or "my network") in the first person. Do not name Dell or frame the work as a Joe-and-Dell partnership in Joe's own copy unless Joe has directed partner-framing for that specific piece. This is the THIRD correction on this theme (July 1 and July 7 preceded it) and a live post shipped violating it on July 16, so it is now a hard ban, enforced first like the AI-tell list above. Internal profile files may still record Dell's tenure as biographical fact; the ban is on public network-attribution, not on the internal record.

## The humanity passes (added July 11, 2026 — distilled from a prompt thread Joe fed the system; procedures, not bans)

Run these on every client- or public-facing draft AFTER the substance is right and BEFORE the final hard-ban check above. They fix what the ban list can't: writing that breaks no rules but still reads machine-made.

1. **Thought-flow pass.** The pacing of ideas is a tell, separate from word choice. Real thinking moves unevenly: quick where the point is obvious, slower where it matters, sometimes blunt. If every paragraph develops at the same controlled speed with the same shape, break the pattern. Cut a windup. Let one point land in six words.
2. **Read-aloud pass.** Read the draft out loud in your head, sentence by sentence. Anywhere you would pause strangely or the rhythm breaks in real speech, rewrite that sentence until it flows like the author talking. This is the cheapest, highest-yield edit in the stack.
3. **Point-of-view pass.** The author takes a position; a neutral observer trying to please everyone is a tell and it is also weak marketing. Find the three sentences that sound most like nobody in particular wrote them and rewrite each as if the author were explaining it to a colleague they respect. Opinions are allowed; that is the point. (Counterpart rule still holds: positions about the MODEL and the market, never about the people across the table.)

**Explicitly rejected (July 11, 2026, do not re-litigate):** manufactured imperfections — planted typos, fake hesitations, deliberately broken sentences. Simulated flaws are their own tell and off-brand for a professional advisor. Authenticity in this system comes from real substance (the fuel engine, firsthand observations) and real opinions, never from costume.
