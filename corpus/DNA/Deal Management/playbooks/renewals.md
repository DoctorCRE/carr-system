# Renewals — Our Playbook (strategy · sourcing · execution · scripts)

> **Doctrine ownership: single writer.** One seat edits this file, the Fable design seat. Every other session, either brain, proposes changes through a guidance-file pull request (AGENTS.md "Guidance changes") or a team-board row, and the seat lands them. Set 2026-08-01 per ORDER 38 (two-writer endgame D3).

*Our unified renewal doctrine (consolidated July 8, 2026 from CARR training + producer practice; sources in the ingestion ledger). Renewals are the most common CRE transaction, the one healthcare providers get exploited on most, and the highest-return/lowest-labor deal type — and the one most agents underweight. Sections marked ⚠️ INTERNAL never go client-facing or into content.*

## 1. Doctrine — why renewals anchor the pipeline
- **Target: renewals ≥ 30% of pipeline** (tracked on the Great-Week Scorecard). Below the bar → source them deliberately, regardless of market.
- **Volume math:** for every 1 new office there are conservatively **10–15 renewals** happening (providers ÷ ~5-yr average lease). Acquisitions usually carry a renewal opportunity too (sellers time sales to lease expiration; ≤2 yrs left → new lease; 3–4 yrs → add term).
- **The iron law: agents who talk about renewals get renewals.** Talk them to every vendor, study club, sponsorship, school, and associate. The line to use everywhere: *"The #1 transaction in commercial real estate is a lease renewal. It's also the #1 transaction where healthcare providers get taken advantage of."* Landlords won't try it on Chipotle; they target independent providers (brilliant clinically, not built for high-dollar negotiation). Say it with conviction — if you haven't convinced yourself, it won't land.
- Renewals **lead into bigger deals**: a 1-year renewal becomes a relocation; a 36-month renewal becomes ground-up construction.

## 2. Sourcing renewal opportunities
- **Mine every building you close in:** get in front of the other healthcare tenants — *"I just went 3–4 rounds with this landlord across 4–5 properties; I have the most accurate market data here — happy to share it, free."* (NDA: never exact terms; the non-binding comparables are fair game.) Every provider in the vicinity becomes a warm cold-call.
- **The free-market-data / lease-evaluation offer is the universal door-opener** — "the gap between a well-negotiated and an average deal is tens to hundreds of thousands; the data's free because we serve the healthcare community either way." Works even years before expiry ("see how you stacked up last time"). If providers consistently won't meet, the pitch is off — fix the pitch, not the audience.
- **CoStar letter-drops:** live in CoStar on "dental office / medical office," pull rent rolls of buildings with **tenants <2 years from expiry**, letter-drop them. B/C-class office vacancy = tenants wanting to upgrade to Class-A medical. Spot a for-sale building on tour → rent roll → letter-drop. (Method details: `DNA/Research/costar-how-to.md` §8.)
- **The referral bench sources most renewals** — supply reps, contractors, CPAs:
  - **Reps:** bring the free Lease Evaluation ("how do you stack up against your neighbors?") to rep meetings; after every referred win, deliver a savings summary that **credits the rep by name** ("Ryan from the supply house is the one who saved you $94,000, not me") — Amazon can't refer a doctor to CARR; that rep can. (Full craft: `DNA/Network/vendor-relationship-craft.md`.)
  - **Contractors:** train them — every "add an operatory / change the carpet" remodel call gets *"your landlord could pay for this — call Joe."*
  - **CPAs:** the benchmark that arms them — healthy practices run **rent/mortgage at ~6–9% of collections**; CPAs see 12–15% (tough renewal waiting) and 2% (great). Give them savings stories for their quarterly client meetings.
- **Study clubs & associations:** even 20–30 seconds at a podium sources deals ("what would it cost for 30 seconds?"). Details in `DNA/Leads/pipeline-craft.md` (events channel).

## 3. The process, end to end (unchanged in a decade — it works)
1. **Set expectations with BOTH sides first.** Tell the doctor there's a ~25% chance the landlord contacts them directly (coffee-and-donuts visits happen). Coach the script: *"Talk to my broker — I'm just a dentist; that's why I hired him."* Set the same expectation with the landlord.
2. **Engagement (ETL) via the free lease evaluation** — current terms vs. market comps side by side. Often reveals the client escalated far above market over 10 quiet years. **Always get the ORIGINAL lease, not just the last amendment** — HVAC responsibility and buried terms live in the original.
3. **ALWAYS tour options — this is where getting paid on renewals comes from.** Even a sworn never-mover goes in the car for 30 minutes and one property: it creates true posture ("we've seen the market"), and never-movers regularly become movers once they see what exists. Tours also collect proposals → the lease-comparison spreadsheet keeps the decision **factual, not emotional**.
4. **The recommended-counteroffer sheet:** current lease on the left, "where you should be" on the right — aim for **~10–15% total-cost savings** (that band, not 5%, not 40%). Doctors almost always say "send the right column."
5. **The landlord call** (Section 5) — introduce, posture, tell them an LOI is coming, ask only for feedback, get off the phone. **Never negotiate live on that first call.**
6. **Deliver everything to the client on the LOI — including commissions.** A landlord refusing to pay the tenant-rep reads to the client as disrespect and as a preview of how maintenance requests will go. Let that surface on its own.
7. **Commencement reality:** a renewal signed 9 months early starts when the current lease ends — set that expectation early.

## 4. Posture — the whole game
- **Posture = the client holding real, capitalizable options.** Terms track posture strength; the landlord must genuinely fear losing the tenant.
- **The move-out math (educate BOTH sides):** medical space averages **12–18 months to re-lease**; two commissions instead of one; $2–3k of new-lease legal vs. a ~$100 amendment; build-out period + free rent for the replacement tenant who *also* won't overpay. Deliver the conclusion: *"There is no scenario where my client moving out makes you more money."*
- **The Three Deals frame:** Deal 1 = the landlord's dream (direct, raised rate, no concessions) — *"that deal is gone; my client's too educated now."* Deal 2 = genuinely competitive renewal (win-win). Deal 3 = tenant leaves, landlord loses big. *"It's Deal 2 or Deal 3 — Deal 1 is never on the table again."*
- **Negotiate 3–5 properties even when the client wants one.** Landlords compete harder, the client KNOWS nothing was left on the table — and you bank approved best-and-final sheets. **Reuse them:** next similar client → call the same landlords: "can we start where Dr. X's best-and-final left off — and you lost that one on free rent, so push further." ~9 of 10 say yes; skip the six-week horse trade. Do this long enough and you know the market better than CoStar does.
- **Vacancy ≠ weak landlord** (educate the client): a half-empty financed building may be holding for the rate that cash-flows the upper floors. Don't let the client assume vacancy = leverage.
- **Options-to-renew = the safety net** (landlord can't terminate mid-negotiation). **Month-to-month = the danger zone** — everything's exposed; go softer there: singles and doubles, not home runs.

## 5. The landlord call (polite, consultative, short)
- **Open with respect, not aggression:** *"I'm coming to you out of respect — you've owned this property for years. I've been hired to evaluate all of Dr. ___'s options; I was referred after helping colleagues of theirs relocate."*
- **Make the ownership connection:** *"Just like you get depreciation and interest benefits from owning, my client wants those too — so we're genuinely evaluating two other options."* High construction costs objection → financing offsets it (healthcare is a <1% default-rate use).
- **Know the market before dialing** — don't ask $9 where the building markets at $15.
- **Close fast:** *"CARR is a concierge, full-service approach. I asked the client what it would take to stay — good news, I got it — and I'll send it in an LOI. All I ask is your feedback. Have a great day."*

## 6. Client management (3–4 minutes each, and the deal depends on them)
- **Explain posture** — "creating that fear is MY job, not yours."
- **The client never talks to the landlord.** One "if you give me X I'll stay" blows the posture. Script them (Section 3.1).
- **Two analogies that land with doctors:** the house sale (you'd take the higher offer without a thought for the buyer — so will your landlord) and insurance reimbursement (you always want the highest reimbursement — landlords are playing the same game against you).
- **"Do you trust your landlord?" → get them to say "no" out loud** — it primes everything after.
- **Walk the space in person** — detriments (no windows, parking, dated building) are posture ammunition.
- A renewal is the **fastest transaction (~1/5 the time of a startup)** — the few minutes of client prep is the whole risk surface. Unprepped doctors fold on landlord calls.

## 7. ⚠️ INTERNAL — landlord objection scripts (never client-facing, never content)
*Posture for all of these: stay calm · make the argument once · walk the math · give them a clean way to accept · get off the phone.*
- **"I don't work with tenant brokers on renewals."** → You can't tell a tenant they can't have representation. They've asked that all communication go through me — are you saying you won't honor that? If you won't work with the broker they hired, they were right not to trust you.
- **"I didn't hire you; I'm not paying you."** → Of course you didn't — the landlord never hires the tenant's broker. The client won't pay my fee, and expects it factored into any offer. Landlords have paid agents for a hundred years; three other landlords will pay us on the alternatives.
- **"We don't pay on renewals / it's policy."** → If they move out and I bring you a stranger, you'll pay then? So the stranger gets treated better than your tenant of ten years? (Behind "policy": *then change the policy.*)
- **"They'll never leave — moving is expensive."** → It's their chance to own a building — something they've always wanted. New space, no living-through-renovations, better location. The only attractive thing about staying is not coming out of pocket; remove that and staying loses. I keep a list of fully built-out spaces — after this one sits vacant a year, I'm usually the first call to backfill it, and that's much harder with my client's strong practice down the street. Once they pay an architect for a fit plan elsewhere, momentum is hard to stop.
- **"I'll pay a reduced fee."** → Every other landlord pays the full fee, and I'm the only reason a renewal is on the table at all. The client expects the full fee factored in. (And privately: 97% beats 0 — judgment call, don't concede on the spot.)
- **The walk-down-the-path levers:** cost-of-vacancy math ("we can work this out now, or you can live it 9–12 months from now"); the horror-movie backfill (a stripped dental build-out competes with 25 other dated ones); the 6%-vs-3% commission frame ("pay your listing agent 6% later or me 3% now — a 3% fee is a 3% savings"); "many savvy landlords would move forward with this"; never renegotiate the fee live, never split-pay ("accounting doesn't work that way").
- **"Put them on ice":** at ~12 months out, a hard "we never pay" gets *"I'll share that with the client — I think we'll move on, but I'll be in touch."* You hold ~6 months of runway. They call back within ~30 days.

## 8. Judgment & timing
- **Doubles beat home runs.** A couple months free + a couple $/SF saved = done; chasing the maximum invites deal fatigue. On renewals ~$10/SF TI gets carpet + paint + free months — and the client was never getting there alone. Comparison is the thief of joy.
- **Touch renewals ~12 months out** — you're on your toes, the landlord's on their heels. At 4–6 months the leverage flips (no time to move). Two years out only with ground-up-construction posture (which genuinely takes two years).
- **Always CALL — never a long email.** Landlords read three sentences. A call carries urgency and signals the client might really move.
- **Watch for the podcast-investor landlord** (bought on a promise of mailbox money, gets emotional when tenants act like tenants) — don't fight, educate.
