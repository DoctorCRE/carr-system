## Dental

**Trigger:** startup, relocation, or acquisition for a GP or dental specialty (Pedo/Ortho/Endo/OMS/Perio/Prostho).

**Vendor sequence:**
1. **Site selection** — Marketing/Demographics vendor for site data if the market isn't already scoped; ground-up needs a Developer/Investor.
2. **Deal** — Banker/Lender matched to Dental (or general-healthcare where no dental-specific lender exists) + Territory/State; CPA for financials; Attorney for entity/lease review if a startup or acquisition.
3. **Build-out** — Architect/Design, General Contractor, and a dental-specific Supply/Equipment Rep (chairs, sterilization, imaging) — pull all three roughly together since build-out specs drive each other.
4. **Ongoing** — IT Services (imaging/PMS integration), Insurance, Practice Broker/Consultant if this is an acquisition.

**Sizing/build-out flag:** operatory-based sizing (~400 SF/operatory) and per-specialty MEP/parking/code triggers — full detail in `DNA/Reference/dental-vertical-guide.md`. Size the space off Ops count from intake before shortlisting properties.

**Pitch angle:** dental carries the strongest documented tenant-creditworthiness story of any vertical CARR represents — use the guide's Pitch-to-Landlord numbers when the landlord needs convincing on a newer practice.

## Medical

**Trigger:** startup, relocation, or additional location for GP or a medical specialty (internal/family med, peds, ortho, plastics, derm, OBGYN, ENT, urgent care, cardiology, GI, pain mgmt, urology, neuro, nephro, rheum, endo, radiology, oncology, surgery center).

**Vendor sequence:**
1. **Site selection** — this is the vertical where MOB-vs-retail matters most; pull Marketing/Demographics early and weigh the referral-engine logic (is the practice referral-driven → MOB, or walk-in → retail visibility) before shortlisting.
2. **Deal** — Banker/Lender matched to the specific specialty's risk profile where a specialty-aware lender exists, else general-healthcare; CPA; Attorney for entity/lease.
3. **Build-out** — Architect/Design + General Contractor; Supply/Equipment Rep only for imaging-heavy or procedure-heavy specialties (lead-lined imaging, lasers/gas per the guide's per-specialty flags).
4. **Ongoing** — IT Services (EMR-heavy vertical, get this in early not late), Insurance.

**Sizing/build-out flag:** 1,500 SF for the first doctor + 500–1,000 SF each additional; watch for the guide's outlier flags (lead-lined imaging rooms, sample-room bathrooms, big-footprint specialties like surgery centers) before sizing off the generic formula. Full detail in `DNA/Reference/medical-vertical-guide.md`.

**Pitch angle:** no standalone Pitch-to-Landlord section exists for medical in the guide — lean on the referral-engine/site-selection logic itself as the pitch (why THIS location captures the referral pattern) rather than a credit-risk narrative.

## Veterinary

**Trigger:** startup, relocation, or acquisition — Small Animal, Large Animal, Emergency, or Multi-Specialty.

**Vendor sequence:**
1. **Site selection** — smell/sound and parking are live site constraints here in a way other verticals don't have; flag early with whoever's doing site selection.
2. **Deal** — Banker/Lender: **check the vertical AND state split** before naming a name (BofA vet lending splits Dahl=AL / Navarro=FL per `introduction-rules.md`'s vertical-awareness section — this vertical has the clearest documented state-split example in the network). CPA; Attorney for entity/lease.
3. **Build-out** — Architect/Design + General Contractor familiar with the standard vet room list (dual-access exam rooms, separate cat/canine kennels, comfort/euthanasia room, back-door freezer, lead-lined x-ray); vet-specific Supply/Equipment Rep.
4. **Ongoing** — IT Services, Insurance.

**Sizing/build-out flag:** sizing varies by clinic type (Small Animal / Large Animal / Emergency / Multi-Specialty); the standard small-animal room list above is the baseline checklist. Full detail in `DNA/Reference/vet-vertical-guide.md`.

**Pitch angle:** the strongest Pitch-to-Landlord package of any vertical — under 1% historical default, $500k+ typical startup loan sizes, a sell-to-vet-group exit story, plus the smell/sound objection-killers built specifically for this vertical. Lead with this when a landlord is skittish.

## Vision

**Trigger:** startup, relocation, or acquisition — Optometry, Ophthalmology, Vision Therapy, or Optician.

**Vendor sequence:**
1. **Site selection** — **retail is 60%+ of an optometry office's revenue**, so this vertical behaves like retail, not referral-driven MOB: prioritize visibility/frontage over the medical-office instinct that governs the other verticals.
2. **Deal** — Banker/Lender matched to vision where available, else general-healthcare; CPA; Attorney.
3. **Build-out** — Architect/Design (the exam-"Lane"/20-ft-chart-distance layout rule is vision-specific, flag it to whoever's designing) + General Contractor + vision-specific Supply/Equipment Rep.
4. **Ongoing** — IT Services, Insurance.

**Sizing/build-out flag:** the Lane/20-ft-chart-distance rule drives exam-room layout; credential tiers matter for staffing conversations (OD vs MD vs optician vs vision therapist). Full detail in `DNA/Reference/vision-vertical-guide.md`.

**Pitch angle:** the retail-forward framing IS the pitch — position the space to the landlord as a retail tenant with a clinical anchor, not a medical tenant that happens to sell product. That reframe is what makes vision sites competitive against actual retail tenants for the best frontage.

## Other Healthcare (Chiropractic & Physical Therapy)

**Trigger:** startup, relocation, or acquisition — Chiropractic or Physical Therapy (the Therapy vertical).

**Vendor sequence:**
1. **Site selection** — lowest build-out bar of the five verticals; parking/mobility access matters more than medical-office prestige, especially for PT.
2. **Deal** — Banker/Lender (general-healthcare, no dedicated chiro/PT lender documented in the network yet — flag as a coverage gap if this vertical's volume grows); CPA; Attorney.
3. **Build-out** — General Contractor (low-TI is the selling point, don't over-build); Architect only if the space needs real reconfiguration; x-ray/lead-lining Supply/Equipment Rep for chiro specifically.
4. **Ongoing** — IT Services, Insurance.

**Sizing/build-out flag:** chiro — x-ray/lead-lining + the **<15-min-visit → 15-minute-parking** concession trick (a genuine negotiating lever, not just a fact) + a low-TI landlord narrative. PT — **"medical office meets gym"**, open floorplan, referral/imaging proximity, mobility/parking. Full detail (including the Greystone Chiropractic buy-and-build case study) in `DNA/Reference/other-healthcare-vertical-guide.md`.

**Pitch angle:** low-TI is the pitch for both — a landlord gets a tenant that doesn't demand expensive medical infrastructure, which is a real selling point against a dental or medical prospect competing for the same space.
