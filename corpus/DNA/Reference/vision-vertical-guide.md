**The one thing that makes Vision different from every other healthcare branch: it's half retail.** Retail (frames, lenses, sunglasses) is **60%+ of an optometry office's revenue.** That flips the site-selection logic — where referral-driven medical specialists want a low-visibility MOB, vision uses (especially optometry and opticians) behave more like **retail tenants that want visibility and frontage.** Keep that front of mind when matching a vision client to space.

## Universal vision facts (apply across the vision verticals)

- **Four distinct credential tiers — know which one you're representing:**
  - **Optometrist (OD):** 4-year college + **4-year optometry school.** Exams + prescriptions for glasses/contacts + manages/treats eye disease. The flagship vision use.
  - **Ophthalmologist (MD):** medical degree + **4–5 year residency.** Does everything an OD does **plus eye surgery** — which is why the layout adds surgery suites.
  - **Vision Therapist:** an **OD who subspecializes in vision therapy** (eye tracking/teaming, depth perception, focusing, lazy eye, and TBI — stroke/concussion/closed-head trauma).
  - **Optician:** **NOT an eye doctor.** Sells and fits eyewear from an OD's/MD's prescription. Some states license opticians; some let them fit contacts. Effectively a **retail tenant.**
- **The retail duality:** every optometry office runs two businesses — **exams and retail** — and **retail is 60%+ of revenue.** Opticians are almost pure retail.
- **The exam "Lane":** an exam room is called a **Lane**, ≈ **11' × 9'**, **2–3 lanes per doctor.** Hard constraint: **chart distance must be 20 feet** — you need a clear 20-ft sightline in/from the lane (mirror systems can fold this, but flag it in due diligence).
- **Pre-test area:** for glaucoma testing, auto visual field screening, etc.
- **Plumbing:** sinks in or near each exam room.
- **MEP (merged 2026-07-31, sourced research pass):** the lightest MEP profile of the six verticals. **HVAC standard office (1 ton per 350–400 SF)** — exam-lane equipment adds no real load; heavy retail display lighting is worth a small allowance, not a line item. **Electrical 200A single-phase is fully adequate even with an edging lab** — exam-lane gear (phoropter, slit lamp, chair, retinal camera) is all 120V wall-plug; a lens edger maxes ~14A and wants a **dedicated 20A circuit for power consistency, not magnitude** (National Optronics 6ES manual). LOI row shape: standard-office HVAC + 200A single-phase + one dedicated 20A circuit to the optical lab. Every term negotiable; these are the defensible most-common asks.

## The verticals at a glance

| Vertical | SF (single practitioner) | Layout signature | Distinctives to flag |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Optometry (OD)** | 1,500–2,000 SF | 2–3 exam **lanes** + pre-test + **retail frame display** + fitting area + lab + reception/office/lounge | The flagship; **retail = 60%+ of revenue** → wants visibility/frontage; 20-ft chart distance; sinks by each lane |
| **Ophthalmology (MD)** | 1,800–2,300 SF | Everything optometry has **plus 2–3 surgery suites** | Performs **eye surgery**; MD + 4–5 yr residency; still carries a retail frame area |
| **Vision Therapy** (OD subspecialty) | 1,500–2,000 SF | Consult room (not exam lanes) + **large open back area like a PT gym** | **Trampolines / activity stations** to treat eye tracking, lazy eye, TBI/concussion; **may or may not** have retail |
| **Optician** (not a doctor) | 1,500–2,000 SF | **Retail takes the majority of the SF** + open-concept reception | Sells/fits eyewear from OD/MD prescriptions; **patron-accessible bathroom**; essentially a **retail tenant** — treat site selection accordingly |

## Pitch to Landlord — vision (INTERNAL, negotiation — CARR proprietary, from the portal)

> **Internal negotiation content — not client-facing.** Why a landlord should *want* an optometry/vision tenant; used to win TI and free rent.

- **A vision use is one of the most secure a landlord can have.** **Extremely low default rate, even for startups** *(note: the vision pitch says "extremely low" but — unlike the dental and vet pitches — does NOT state a hard <1% figure; don't invent one, see the external note below).* Built-out vision space re-leases fast — often a new lease is executed **before the prior tenant is out.** Optometrists rarely relocate (construction cost).
- **Justifying a large TI:** the TI keeps the client in the best position on their practice loan and funds the equipment the intended vision care requires. Money spent on the space is money that can't fund success.
- **Downside is still fine for the landlord:** on relocation or the small chance of default, the landlord holds a desirable, built-out asset. On default the **bank's only fallback is equipment worth pennies on the dollar** (well over half the loan went to leasehold + working capital) — yet **banks keep lending to optometry**, which itself signals the bank's confidence in the practice's success.
- **Why these doctors don't default:** rather than bankruptcy, a struggling optometrist **sells the "bones" of the office to an optometry group** — there are **billions in enterprise money** in the optometry acquisition market. The landlord ends up with a *stronger* tenant.
- **Net-worth objection:** a young doctor's negative net worth is overwhelmingly **student-loan** debt, and banks still compete hard to lend (no collateral recourse) despite it.

## Creditworthiness — external corroboration (NOT CARR portal; cited)

*The vision pitch asserts "billions in enterprise money" in optometry but gives no numbers. Here's the documented backing — use with attribution:*
- Private equity is deep in eye care: **16 of the 25 largest healthcare PE investors** (per PitchBook) hold optometry/ophthalmology stakes, and PE works with **~8% of U.S. ophthalmologists** (Dr. Robert Wiggins, AAO president). Named platforms: **EyeCare Partners** (300 ophthalmologists + 700 optometrists across 19 states; bought by Partners Group in 2019 for **$2.2 billion**), **Retina Consultants of America** ($350M formation, 2020), **MyEyeDr, Midwest Vision Partners, EyeSouth Partners.** Source: KFF Health News, "Private Equity Sees the Billions in Eye Care." https://kffhealthnews.org/news/article/private-equity-ophthalmology-eye-care-high-profit-procedures/
- On the low-default claim: optometry isn't broken out in the SBA cohort data cited in `medical-vertical-guide.md`, but healthcare practices broadly sit among the lowest-default SBA borrowers (dentists ~3.76% / physicians ~4.75% lifetime charge-off vs ~7.5% all-industry). Treat the vision pitch's "extremely low default" as directionally consistent with that, not as a specific published optometry figure.
