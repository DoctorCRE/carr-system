## The universal CARR angle (runs through every category)

Most of these vendors **cannot close their own sale until the client secures space**, and every one of them serves practices with lease renewals, relocations, or purchases coming. So the cross-path is always the same: (1) we move their deals forward faster / save their client money, (2) **"have you ever asked your clients when their lease is up?"** turns their existing book into shared opportunities, (3) ~**20-30% of the renewals we take on end up relocating or purchasing** (vs. renewing) = more work for them too. Reassure them we keep pointing the client back to them throughout and never cross-refer their business away. Bring a **Savings Summary that credits the referring vendor** (strengthens THEIR client bond), and close with the **Free Evaluation Card / a customized Free Evaluation page** on the CARR site. For CPAs, Financial Planners, Attorneys, Consultants: bring a **lease-vs-lease and lease-vs-purchase comparison** — they like data and it positions us as analytical, not a "pushy broker."

## 1. Equipment Reps (buildout equipment & cabinetry)
Great source of **project deals** (start-ups, relocations, additional offices). They can't sell their product until the client secures space, so they spend nights/weekends hunting property for clients and can't move forward without it — when we plug in as a trusted advisor we take work off their plate.
- **Value add:** with buildout costs pressuring the equipment budget, a client may only equip a couple of rooms; if we negotiate aggressive TI, that frees budget for more equipment/technology.
- **Conversation starters:** How long in the role / did you take over a book or build from scratch? How many new accounts/year are you expected to add? Have you ever asked clients about their lease? Do you work a specific territory? Any newsletters (would you add real estate content)? How big is your team — can you connect me with the branch manager?
- **Meeting playbook:** find the natural tie into how we cross-paths, give the 30-second CARR commercial (posture, multiple options), lean on renewals, bring a Savings Summary crediting the rep, mention the 20-30% relocate/purchase = more merch/equipment for them.
- **Conflicts/overlap:** Lenders (in-house equipment financing can compete with a lender, though most reps don't care how it's financed).

### How the distributor channel actually works (added 2026-07-31, from open-source research on the Brandvold C-153 referral)

*Researched after Gabriel Arroyo, a Patterson Equipment & CAD/CAM Specialist, introduced Dr. Mike Brandvold to Dell and stayed in the deal. Sources: Patterson's own job postings and services pages, Cirrus Consulting Group, industry cost data. Merged here rather than filed separately, per the growth-by-merge rule.*

**The two roles, and they are genuinely different jobs at the same company.**

| | **Territory Rep** (merchandise / supply) | **Equipment Specialist** (buildout) |
|---|---|---|
| Sells | Consumables, small tech, business solutions | Capital equipment, cabinetry, CAD/CAM, imaging |
| Owns | The account, day to day, all year | The **project**: new builds, relocations, expansions |
| Cadence | Recurring, high frequency | Episodic, high value |
| In a build | Brings the equipment specialist in | **Runs it** |
| Paid | Commission | Commission |

**The equipment specialist's real job is bigger than selling boxes.** Patterson's own posting has them "initiate and manage new and remodeled dental office design projects," prepare the project schedule, monitor the build to completion, "evaluate project plans and blueprints, coordinate with designers and sub/contractors to inspect work," and **draw the plans themselves** in design software. Patterson backs them with an in-house Office Design team covering everything from evaluating a chosen location to space planning and interior finishes.

**So in a project deal the equipment specialist is a de facto project manager, not a vendor waiting on us.** Treat them accordingly: they hold the floor plan, the operatory count, and the schedule, which makes them the fastest route to a client's real space program. Ask them, rather than waiting on the doctor.

**⭐ The economics, which is the part worth internalizing.** A dental operatory runs roughly **$75,000 to $175,000** installed. A six-operatory office is therefore **$450,000 to $1M+** of equipment on one project, all of it commission to one person who is paid on commission only.

**That is why an equipment rep hunts property on nights and weekends,** and it is the sharpest version of the incentive this file already describes. Their payday is gated entirely on the doctor securing space. **Our interests are not merely compatible with theirs, they are identical, and we should say so out loud.** Compare the merchandise rep, whose interest in the same project is a slower, smaller lift in recurring consumables.

**⚠️ Qualifies the existing "lead with the merchandise rep" guidance** (`vendor-relationship-craft.md` Part B, Q&A). That rule reflects the norm, where the doctor treats the local merch rep as ringleader. **The Brandvold deal inverted it: the equipment specialist sourced the client, made the introduction, and is running the project.** On a project deal (start-up, second office, relocation) the equipment specialist may well be the ringleader. Lead with the merch rep on an ESTABLISHED account; lead with the equipment specialist on a PROJECT.

### 🔴 The competitor nobody had recorded: Cirrus Consulting Group

**Patterson's official "Build" program routes lease negotiation to Cirrus Consulting Group, and Henry Schein promotes a lease-negotiation service too.** Cirrus lists both distributors as partners, alongside the ADA, AGD, Bank of America and TD Bank. **This is the incumbent real-estate answer inside the distributor channel, and it is national.**

**What Cirrus is, from their own site:** lease review and negotiation for healthcare practices, "created by doctors for doctors," national and remote. They negotiate terms and expose bad lease clauses.

**What Cirrus is NOT, and this is the wedge:**
- **Leases only.** No purchase transactions anywhere in their offer. **A doctor buying a building or land is entirely outside their lane.**
- **They do not source property.** They help "evaluate opportunities, compare spaces," meaning they work on a space the doctor already found. No site selection, no market sweep, no off-market access.
- **National and remote.** No local submarket knowledge, no relationships with local listing agents, no ability to walk a building.
- **No disclosed real estate licensure**, and no fee structure published.

**How to hold this in a conversation with a Patterson or Schein rep,** without attacking a partner their employer promotes: Cirrus reads a lease the doctor has already been handed. We find the options in the first place, create competition between landlords, and we handle purchases and land, which they do not touch at all. On a second-office purchase like Brandvold there is no overlap to argue about.

**Standing implication:** corporate is pointing reps at Cirrus. Every local equipment-rep relationship is therefore a relationship held against a corporate default, which makes those relationships more valuable and more fragile than they look. Dell's ten years with Gabriel is the moat. Treat it as one.

## 2. Attorneys
A vital part of every CARR agent's network — we advise every client to use a good real estate attorney, so you want **multiple** relationships. Target **real estate** attorneys (not family/litigation); healthcare-specialized RE attorneys are even better.
- **Value add:** strong reciprocal partner. They review leases and purchase due-diligence, critical dates; we bring market comps, current concession packages, and multi-option negotiation their clients need but they don't do. When their clients ask them to negotiate a renewal or acquisition, we help on the parts they can't.
- **Conversation starters:** Tell me about your firm / do you specialize? How many leases do you review a year? Purchase agreements? Do you get involved in practice transitions (how do you handle the RE — lease assignment/extension)? Do you connect clients to financing/lending/accounting/insurance vendors? Newsletters? Present at universities/associations/CE events?
- **Meeting playbook:** 30-second commercial with a highlight on **lease renewals** (they serve existing practices with renewals to review), bring a sample RFP/LOI so they see what we negotiate up front (so they don't retread deal points), close with the Free Evaluation Card + a customized Free Evaluation page.
- **Conflicts/overlap:** Practice brokers; **Real estate brokers — some attorneys try to handle the RE brokerage side themselves (they compete with CARR)**; other attorneys (some compete, some are all-referral).

## 3. CPAs
Connected to many healthcare clients (accounting, bookkeeping, payroll up to cost-segregation studies and practice acquisitions) and one of the **most trusted advisors** to the doctor — when a CPA recommends, the client acts.
- **Value add:** a CPA's job is finding efficiencies/cost savings; our ability to cut tens-to-hundreds of thousands off a lease or purchase is a beneficial added service line they can offer.
- **Conversation starters:** Do you "benchmark" clients vs. national averages? Meet every client at year-end? How often do you ask when leases are due? Do you connect clients to financing/lending vendors? Assisting in any practice transitions (buyers or sellers — how do you handle the lease assignment/extension the lender requires)? Newsletters? Present at events?
- **Meeting playbook:** 30-second commercial highlighting renewals; bring a **blank lease-vs-lease and lease-vs-purchase comparison** so the CPA sees we provide high-level data for informed (not emotional) decisions — they like numbers, it shows we're analytical (don't leave the comparisons with them). Close with the Free Evaluation Card.
- **Conflicts/overlap:** Practice brokers; IT (occasionally); Consultants; Financial planners.

## 4. Financial Planners
Both a source of deals and a resource for our clients. Some FPs focus on **schools/residency programs** — those are the targets, since they're in with graduating classes and past students now starting or acquiring a practice.
- **Value add:** FPs help clients limit overhead/liability, so lease-renewal negotiations are a great discussion point — the more a client saves on lease/purchase, the more they invest with the FP for retirement. We save them time and money on one of their costliest line items.
- **Conversation starters:** Tell me about your firm / specialty? How often do you meet clients (quarterly, annually)? How often do you ask when leases are due? Do you connect clients to financing/lending/accounting/insurance vendors? Newsletters? Present at events?
- **Meeting playbook:** 30-second commercial on renewals; if they present at schools, discuss how we evaluate **acquisitions vs. start-ups** and help associates looking to own. Bring the lease-vs-lease / lease-vs-purchase comparison (FPs, like CPAs, like data). Close with the Free Evaluation Card.
- **Conflicts/overlap:** Insurance reps; CPAs (some provide financial planning, some don't).

## 5. General Contractors
Part of most project deals (start-ups, relocations, expansions) and a great source of market intel (construction costs, permitting timelines, electrical/HVAC/space hurdles). Some run **Design-Build** firms (in-house design + architects).
- **Value add:** we bring them onto new projects; we clarify what the landlord is responsible for vs. what the GC must include in their bid; when a GC picks up a client directly they can't sell services until space is secured, so we move that along; aggressive TI negotiation stretches the client's budget = a larger project for the GC.
- **Conversation starters:** Tell me about your business / specialty area? How many healthcare buildouts/buildings completed? Buildout vs. ground-up focus? Design-build, or who do you work with on the architect side? Ballpark build-out cost per SF for a dental/vet/medical office in this market? Permitting timeline with the county/city (county-to-county differences, parking code)? Connected to any equipment reps in the market?
- **Meeting playbook:** 30-second commercial (projects, start-ups, expansions), bring a lease-vs-purchase comparison (helps clients evaluate options → creates new projects for everyone), ask about their buildout clients from 5/7/10 years ago (now may be time for a purchase/relocation/renewal). Close with the Free Evaluation Card.
- **Conflicts/overlap:** Developers or landlords who want to provide construction.

## 6. Consultants
Wide playing field — some focus on start-ups, others on taking existing practices to the next level; some are "in-and-out" for a season, others connected for years. Know **how they get involved in the client's real estate (if at all).**
- **Value add:** help their start-up/relocation clients walk the process with correct posture/strategy; help their existing clients find lease efficiencies at renewal. Many consultants talk about us as their "real estate arm" or an added service line they offer.
- **Conversation starters:** Tell me about your business / area of consulting? Do you typically get into real estate with clients (used RE brokers before? what factors matter when advising on a lease)? Do you have a consistent circle of vendors? Focused on a specific region? Present at universities/associations/study clubs?
- **Meeting playbook:** 30-second commercial (projects, start-ups, expansions), make clear we're a **no-cost added service** they can offer clients; show lease comparisons / purchase-vs-lease to highlight our market expertise. Offer to build a customized page for them to use with their clients. Close with the Free Evaluation Card.
- **Conflicts/overlap:** Architects; CPAs; Attorneys; Financial planners.

## 7. Architects
Part of most project deals and often the **hub of the entire project** as it takes shape (design, layout, clinic size). Tenant projects: fit plans, troubleshoot with equipment rep/GC/owner. Ground-up: work with civil engineer, planning commission, city council, GC for approvals. A terrific resource on ADA, parking, zoning, site-specific questions.
- **Value add:** we bring them onto new projects; our landlord negotiations benefit them (e.g., negotiating for the landlord to pay for a fit plan); large TI allowances give the architect more design freedom; we liaise between owner and architect until the project is underway.
- **Conversation starters:** Tell me about your business / specialty? How many healthcare projects? What do you charge for a fit plan? Which GCs do you work with most closely? Ballpark build-out cost per SF for a dental/vet/medical office here? Work with prefab systems (e.g., DIRTT)? Collaborate with out-of-state consultants that provide design services? How often should a client refresh their space?
- **Meeting playbook:** 30-second commercial (projects, start-ups, expansions), bring a lease-vs-purchase comparison (evaluating options creates new projects), ask about buildout clients from 5/7/10 years ago (time for purchase/relocation/renewal). Close with the Free Evaluation Card.
- **Conflicts/overlap:** Consultants or equipment reps that provide design services; Design/build contractors.

## 8. IT Companies
Hardware, software, cloud for practices — very few operate without it. Usually one of the **last** vendors to learn about a project, but they touch a high volume of practices and can uncover a practice looking to relocate/renew.
- **Value add:** by the timing/nature of our interaction we have significant influence on which downstream partners clients choose — early support is a big win for IT companies (usually battling competitors). Big TI allowance increases the client's ability to upgrade technology; getting IT involved early lets them run cabling before drywall.
- **Conversation starters:** Tell me about your business / focus? Geographic footprint / how many clients in that area? Per-service-call or monthly service fee? Own service techs or contract out? Cloud solutions? Do you provide HIPAA or cybersecurity training to practice owners/teams? What upgrades are practices making right now?
- **Meeting playbook:** 30-second commercial (projects, start-ups, expansions), help them incorporate lease conversations with existing clients, talk TI allowance + technology upgrades at renewal. Close with the Free Evaluation Card.
- **Conflicts/overlap:** Distributors selling computer equipment or IT services; CPAs or other vendors providing IT.

## 9. Practice Brokers
Work with buyers and sellers on practice sales and partnership buy-ins — almost always represent the **seller**. Paid a % on both the practice and the real estate (if applicable), which makes many **reluctant** to introduce a real estate broker. Often collaborate with CPAs/attorneys but may provide those services in-house.
- **Value add:** few know how to properly value real estate (they rely on appraisers/RE brokers) or communicate price justification to a buyer. We solve both by acting as **transaction brokers** to establish a price range and facilitate the RE purchase; when the selling practice has a lease, we negotiate the best terms on the new lease (or assignment/extension) for the buyer — keeping the transaction intact via better cash flow or needed TI. They need to trust **we will not blow up their deal before they bring us in.**
- **Conversation starters:** In what states do you list practices (where's the majority)? How do you determine fair market value / do you handle valuation yourself or with a CPA/attorney? Handle sale documents yourself or outside attorney? Ever had a transaction fall apart over a lease or RE purchase? How do you determine a lease rate/purchase price when RE is involved?
- **Meeting playbook:** 30-second commercial on how we ensure a practice purchase overcomes RE issues (lease assignment, cold feet, appraisal shortfalls); we take the heavy lifting off their plate so they focus on the practice side + other listings; we handle the RE and **they still receive half the commission in most cases.** Close with the Free Evaluation Card.
- **Conflicts/overlap:** CPAs; Attorneys; Consultants; Distributors who broker practices.

## 10. Insurance Reps
Every practice owner has insurance (professional/malpractice, property/casualty, disability, life). Some reps focus professional, some personal, some both. Typically one of the **last** to learn about a project but they touch a high volume of practices and can surface a relocation/renewal.
- **Value add:** very few startup doctors have picked an insurance person until we recommend one; existing practices may reconsider at relocation. Many clients need the insurance portion of the lease reviewed before execution but fail to do it in time (rep works under a time crunch) — we get them in contact early.
- **Conversation starters:** Tell me about your business / types of insurance you provide? Where do you send clients for insurance you don't provide? How long to obtain a policy / what's the process? What gaps do most clients have? How often do you talk with clients / discuss their lease? Do you keep copies of existing clients' leases?
- **Meeting playbook:** 30-second commercial highlighting how we introduce them and provide value at renewals; bring a Savings Summary Letter showing how we push benefit/value back to the insurance rep. Close with the Free Evaluation Card.
- **Conflicts/overlap:** Financial planners; Other insurance reps (some provide competing coverage, some don't).
